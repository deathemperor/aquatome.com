Relevant Information:
Description: Dorsal spines (total): 1; Dorsal soft rays (total): 4 - 5; Anal soft rays: 9 - 10; Vertebrae: 35 - 36. Differs from other species by having a wide elliptical cranial fontanel; short postcleithral process not reaching vertical through origin of dorsal fin. The only other Tatia with a variable colouration is Tatia galaxias, but this species differs from T. galaxias by its short postcleithral process ; its small eye, 21.4-26.3% HL and its long snout 38.8-44.3% HL. Can be further diagnosed by the following characters: nasal ossified with wide medial flanges partially sutured to lateral margin of mesethmoid and caudal-fin lobes of mature females with same length, but mature males with elongated upper lobe are other distinguishing features. A iii,6-7; V i5. Post-Weberian vertebrae 35-36. Colouration: Body colouration variable, usually with elliptical spots along sides of body, large specimens with faint spots; or colour pattern uniformly pale brown; and caudal fin with small spots or pale brown. Sexual Differences: The anal fin is the key to the sexual dimorphism of this genus, if you think of the male and female of most livebearer fish (Goodeidae family) and you wil not be too far away with this assumption. The female has a normal anal fin but the males are modified into a copulatory organ with the first and second ray thickened and longer, it is thought that the male uses this to clasp the female during the spawning embrace. Etymology: intermedia: intermediate, with eyes larger than T. aulopygia but smaller than Centromochlus heckelii, its presumed congener at the time.

IUCN Red List Tatia intermedia is described for Brazil, occurring in the lower third of the Amazon basin (states of Amazonas, Pará, and Tocantins), in the Tocantins-Araguaia system, and coastal rivers of Pará and Amapá. It also occurs in rivers of Guyana, Suriname, and French Guiana. The species is frequent and abundant in the locations where it has been recorded. There is no information on the connectivity between the subpopulations in Brazil and the rivers of the Guianas. As no significant threats were identified, Tatia intermedia was categorised as Least Concern (LC). (IUCN 2022). Reference:
