Description: Denticetopsis iwokrama is most similar to D. seducta, a species of the central and western portions of the Amazon basin. The two species demonstrate a distinct difference in the number of caudal and total vertebrae and the pigmentation pattern of the pectoral fin. Etymology: The specific name iwokrama: Named for the Iwokrama rainforest project in the region of Guyana, during which the holotype was collected.

IUCN Red List Denticetopsis iwokrama is only known from the type locality in the Siparuni River basin, and two additional records from Kuyuwini River, tributary to Essequibo basin, and Takutu River near to Moco-Moco village, in Guyana. The extent of occurrence (EOO) is estimated at 35,041 km2, and the area of occupancy (AOO) at 2,936 km2. However, it is suspected that the species has a larger distribution than currently known, occurring in other tributaries of the basin in similar habitats of small creeks. Although it is known from few localities, the overall distribution seems to be preserved and there are no apparent impacts on the species. Thus, Denticetopsis iwokrama is assessed as Least Concern. Further survey work is needed to collect species in more localities in order to better understand its ecology, population and the distribution (IUCN 2022). Reference:
