Description: Dorsal spines (total): 1; Dorsal soft rays (total): 7 - 8; Anal spines: 0; Anal soft rays: 13 - 15; Vertebrae: 34 - 36. Slender body (body depth at anus 14.1-15.9% SL); short adipose fin base (length of adipose fin base 12.8-16.4% SL); large eyes (eye diameter 12.3-16.1% HL). Colouration: Body brown with three rows of cream-coloured spots located above, along and below lateral line. Etymology: The genus name Nanobagrus: Latin, nannus = small + Mozarabic bagre, Greek pagros = a fish, Dentex sp. The specific name stellatus: from the Latin for starred or spotted, in reference to the pattern of cream-coloured spots on the body.

6.5-7.0. IUCN Red List This species is known from the Batang Hari drainage in Sumatra, and the Pahang and Endau river drainages in the Malay Peninsula. Potential threats include habitat degradation resulting from deforestation due to logging and land conversion for plantation agriculture. However, the direct impact of these threats on its habitat extent and population status are unknown. Because of the broad distribution of this species, it is unlikely that the number of locations is less than five. Pervasive threats are considered unlikely to drive this species to critically endangered or extinct within 1-2 generations, Therefore, Nanobagrus stellatus is assessed as Least Concern (IUCN 2020).
