Clarias pachynema Boulenger, 1903

Relevant Information:
Description: Dorsal spines (total): 0; Dorsal soft rays (total): 81-95; Anal spines: 0; Anal soft rays: 63 - 76; Vertebrae: 65 - 67. Diagnosis: head length 19.6-24.6% SL (Ref. 81644). Head pointed in dorsal outline; snout short; tooth plates small; eyes dorsally located; "dermosphenotic" and supraorbital bones joined in specimens over 80 mm SL. Frontal fontanella long and narrow ("knife-shaped"). Occipital process long and oval-shaped. Distance between occipital process and dorsal-fin base 4.3-10.5% SL (m = 8.1). Gill rakers long, slender and distantly set, 11-19 on 1st gill arch. Suprabranchial organ well developed. Pectoral spine serrated on both sides, strongly on posterior face, weakly on anterior face. 65-67 vertebrae. Habitat: Found in the River Sanaga in Cameroon. From there the distribution extends southwards and includes the Ogowe drainage in Gabon and the central Congo system in Congo (Seegers 1996). Colouration: Preserved specimens uniformly dark brown on back and sides, and yellowish-brown on belly. Vertical series of small white spots on flanks. Etymology: The specific name pachynema: pachys, thick; nema, thread, presumably referring to longer barbels compared to C. laeviceps. Remarks: The species is widespread without any major threats. Therefore, it is assessed as Least Concern (IUCN 2020).
