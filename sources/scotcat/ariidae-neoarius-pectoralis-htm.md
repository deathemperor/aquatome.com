Neoarius pectoralis (Kailola, 2000)

Relevant Information:
Habitat: Found in coastal waters, estuaries and rivers within tidal influence; among mangroves. Description: Head large, eye large, head shield granular with a long narrow median groove; Snout acutely pointed, lips fleshy and crenulated, lower jaw squarish; Inner margin of pectoral-fin spine with large flattened serrations; Caudal fin with slender pointed lobes. Dorsal spines (total): 1; Dorsal soft rays (total): 7; Anal spines: 0; Anal soft rays: 18 - 22; Vertebrae: 51. Palatal teeth in two oblique, oval patches; one on each side of palate, well separated. Snout acute; lips fleshy, crenulate; lower jaw truncate. Head shield granular; dorsomedian head groove lanceolate, narrow distally. Eye large, 17-28% HL. Large, flattened serrae along inner margin of pectoral spine; caudal fin lobes slender, acute. Rakers present along posterior face of all gill arches. Colouration: Distinct, blackish margin on dorsal, pectoral and inner caudal fins. Etymology: The genus name Neoarius: Neo means "new" in latin and Arius is Derived from the Greek word Apelos (pronounced Araios) which means "sanctified by the god of war", and refers to the bony structure extending from the skull covering to the powerful thorn of the first dorsal fin ray. The specific name 'pectoralis' is from Latin meaning 'shoulder', in reference to the strong serrations along the inner margin of the pectoral-fin spine. Remarks: A large fork-tailed catfish with venomous spines that may inflict a very painful wound.
