Malapterurus oguensis Sauvage, 1879
Image contributors to this species:
Brian Sidauskas (1) Joe Cutler (2)
ScotCat Sources:
Other Sources:
Relevant Information:
Description: Anal spines: 0; Anal soft rays: 8 - 10; Vertebrae: 38 - 40. Diagnosis: tooth patches narrow; vertically based pectoral fin positioned near body mid-depth; 6-7 branched caudal-fin rays; 8 (rarely 7 or 9) pectoral-fin rays; 3 ventral unbranched caudal-fin rays; dorsum, flank and head unspotted; caudal saddle and bar pattern intensely expressed with wide, dark and well defined saddle that extends onto the anal fin, with no interruption between saddle and anal-fin pigment fields (in all other species with a caudal saddle there is a clear break in pigment between the saddle and the anal fin pigmentation); venter unpigmented; 4-14 gill-rakers; 38-40 vertebrae. Etymology: The specific name oguensis: The specific epithet is geographic referring to the Ogowe (Ogôoué) River where the types originated.
