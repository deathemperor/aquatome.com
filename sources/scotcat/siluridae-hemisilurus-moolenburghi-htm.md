Relevant Information:
Description: As with all three species in this genus, H. heterorhynchus, H. mekongensis, and H. moolenburghi, they do not have a dorasal fin and sport two barbels. Sexual Differences: The females of H. moolenburghi have longer barbels. Etymology: The specific name moolenburghi: In honour of Pieter Eliza Moolenburgh (1872–1944), ethnographer and government official in the Dutch East Indies, who gave a large collection of fishes from Sumatra to the authors, presumably including holotype of this one.

IUCN Red List This species is reported only from the Batang Hari drainage in Sumatra and the Kapuas River drainage in Borneo. Potential threats include habitat degradation resulting from deforestation due to logging and land conversion for plantation agriculture. However, the direct impact of these threats habitat extent and population status are unknown (because the effects of anthropogenic threats on the populations of many tropical large-river fish species is very poorly studied). Although the extent of occurrence (EOO) falls within the range for a threatened category, there is insufficient information on the direct impact of existing threats and their impact on population trend.
