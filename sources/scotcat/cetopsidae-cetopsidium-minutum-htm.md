Cetopsidium minutum (Eigenmann, 1912)

Relevant Information:
Description: Dorsal soft rays (total): 6 - 6; Anal soft rays: 23 - 24; Vertebrae: 39 - 40. The combination of the presence of a dorsal fin with an ossified spinelet and the presence of pectoral and dorsal-fin spines distinguishes the species of Cetopsidium from all other genera in the Cetopsinae. The possession of a lateral line extending to over the posterior portion of the base of the anal fin but falling short of the caudal peduncle is also unique to Cetopsidium within the Cetopsinae. This species differs from all other species of Cetopsidium in the length of the pelvic fin (tip of fin reaching posteriorly to the anal-fin origin versus only reaching or falling short of the vent, respectively). Etymology: The specific name minutum: Latin for very small, referring to length of type specimen, 22 mm long.

IUCN Red List Cetopsidae minutum is known from Potaro drainage at Kuribronhg River, Cuyuni River, and the middle portion of the Essequibo River, in Guyana. The extent of occurrence (EOO) is 28,784 km2, and the area of occupancy (AOO) is 9,516 km2. Furthermore, it is expected that species has a larger distribution than currently known, occurring in other tributaries of the basin in similar habitats of the rivers. Further survey work is needed to collect specimens in more localities in order to better understand its real range of distribution. The only impact observed in the peripheral distributional region is related to the presence of gold mining in the Kuribrong and Potaro Rivers, but not in the localities of the specimens recorded. Due to the relatively expanded distribution of the species in the Essequibo River basin, these local impacts are therefore unlikely to affect its long-term persistence. Notwithstanding, the area should be monitored in order to access the real habitat conditions and status of the population. Thus, Cetopsidium minutum is assessed as Least Concern (LC) due to its distribution and the lack of any known potential major threats. However, this species is not well known, as well as being uncommon and so further research should be carried out to ensure it is not in decline (IUCN 2022). Reference:
