Relevant Information:
Marceniuk et al (2007) placed this species in the genus, Arius, due to the moderate size of its adipose fin and the lateral line bifurcated at the caudal region, although it did not have the accessory tooth plates which places it in this genus. Description: Three pairs of barbels, the maxillary barbels shorter than head. No teeth on plate. Strong dorsal spine, as long as head without snout. Pectoral spines as long as the dorsal spine. Habitat: It is found in the rivers and estuaries of South Asia. Colouration: Body bluish dark superiorly and lighter below with yellowish fins. Lower caudal lobe tinted gray with a dark spot on adipose fin. Etymology: The specific name jatius: latinisation of Jat from Jat gagora, local Bengali name for this catfish in India [a noun in apposition, often incorrectly spelled jatia]. Remarks: Some online searches still have this species listed as Cephalocassis jatia.

7.0-8.5. IUCN Red List There is insufficient information on the distribution, population (and its trends), and biology of Cephalocassis jatiaius (Arius jatius). Although this species is known to be common for part of its range (Irrawaddy River drainage), uncertainties regarding the taxonomic status of the Ganges-Brahmaputra and Irrawaddy populations and the lack of population information of this species in the Ganges-Brahmaputra system makes it impossible to accurately assess this species, and it is considered Data Deficient, but needs updating (IUCN 2010).
