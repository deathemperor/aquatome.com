Arius maculatus (Thunberg, 1792)

Relevant Information:
Description: Strong venomous dorsal and pectoral spines provide protection for the fish. Dorsal spines (total): 1; Dorsal soft rays (total): 7; Anal soft rays: 16 - 30. Head shield somewhat rugose; deep and long median fontanelle groove. Habitat: Occurs in inshore waters and estuaries. Occasionally form schools. Caught mainly with set bag nets and bamboo stake traps. Air bladders are exported as isinglass used by the wine industry. Reproduction: Males incubate eggs in buccal cavity. During incubation, males starve which sometimes make them resort to swallowing one or two eggs probably to maintain basal metabolism. Early hatching embryos commence feeding on inhaled particles by the female when still in possession of large yolk. Diet: Feeds on invertebrates and small fishes. Etymology: The specific name maculatus: Meaning spotted.
