This form is also traded as Betta ocellata, and it has not been settled whether the two are the same species.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Long and slender with a large, blunt head. Fully grown males have a larger body than females. Fins: Rounded caudal fin. Fully grown males have longer pelvic fins. Scaling: Adult males develop full iridescent plating on the cheeks and gill plates. Females sometimes show iridescence on the cheeks too. Coloration: Brown body with strong iridescence on the cheeks and lighter iridescence on the body and fins. Females are duller. Background: Island population traded under its collection locality; not yet matched to a described species. Care GuideTemperament: Aggressive. Best kept in a species-only tank, since they will eat any fish that fits in their mouth. Give a pair plenty of caves and cover to break line of sight. Recommended 1 pair per 10 gal, or 20 gal for breeding.Max Size: 3.5 inchesTDS: 60 to 100pH: 5.0 to 7.0Temperature: 72 to 82°F (they prefer cooler water, but warmer water brings out color and breeding behavior)Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets.Health: In our experience, fish in this complex are very prone to ich and velvet during the first few weeks after import, so watch new arrivals closely.Breeding: Paternal mouthbrooder. The female collects the eggs during spawning and passes them to the male, who holds them in his mouth for about 2 to 3 weeks. Keep the tank quiet and leave a holding male alone, since a disturbed male may swallow or spit out the brood. He releases fully formed fry that can eat baby brine shrimp right away.
