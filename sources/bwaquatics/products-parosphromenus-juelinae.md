It is known only from a few forest streams and swamps in a single river system on Bangka Island, Indonesia. Mining, oil palm and settlement have degraded much of the lowland habitat on Bangka. It was once exported under a false "P. deissneri Belitung" label.Learn more from our friends at the Parosphromenus Project.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Small and slender. Fins: Rounded caudal fin. The base of the unpaired fins is black, and the inner iridescent band is broken into an arc of irregular blotches. The pelvic filament is light blue. Coloration: Dark brown stripes along the body, with intense red blotches covering the rest of the body up to the base of the fins. The red flanks set it apart from P. bintan and P. deissneri. Habitat (wild Parosphromenus juelinae): Shaded forest streams and peat swamps with clear, tea-colored water and dense Cryptocoryne and Utricularia. Care GuideTemperament: Peaceful and shy. Keep a pair in 5 gal, or a group of 3 to 6 in 10 gal with plenty of caves and leaf litter. They can share a tank with tiny, calm blackwater fish such as Boraras, or with small mouthbrooding bettas like albimarginata and channoides. Never mix different Parosphromenus species, since they can hybridize.Max Size: About 1.4 inches (not yet published; estimated from close relatives)TDS: 0 to 50pH: 4.0 to 6.0Temperature: 72 to 82°FDiet: Micropredator. Wild-caught fish usually take only live food. We currently feed live baby brine shrimp, daphnia and blackworms.Breeding: Cave spawner with a horizontal to head-down courtship. Care follows the usual licorice gourami standard, though early reports suggest it may be a little harder to breed than most. The male guards the eggs and larvae until the fry swim free.
