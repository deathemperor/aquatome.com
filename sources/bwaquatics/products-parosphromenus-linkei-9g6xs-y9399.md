It comes from southern Central Kalimantan, Borneo, where it was first found near Palangan in the Mentaya catchment and later around Palangkaraya, giving it the largest known range in the genus. It is a lean, low-backed species, and together with Parosphromenus ornaticauda (its "twin species") it forms a small group with its own courtship style. Breeding is unreliable, so it is suited to experienced keepers.Learn more from our friends at the Parosphromenus Project.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Lean, with a low back and an oval cross-section, unlike the deeper-bodied licorice gouramis. Fins: Dorsal and anal fins black with red spots, with white iridescent edges that vary by locality. Pelvic fins are clear, and the caudal fin is almost colorless to dark blackish. Coloration: Males in breeding dress have a very dark to black lower body and a yellow to yellow-brown upper body. Females usually keep a striped pattern and never fully match the male's colors. Similar Species: Females can be confused with Parosphromenus ornaticauda, and the juveniles of the two are nearly impossible to tell apart. Habitat (wild Parosphromenus parvulus): Shallow, leaf-filled pools and slow forest streams of very soft, acidic blackwater. Care GuideTemperament: Peaceful and shy. Males cover more ground than most licorice gouramis during their courtship display. Best kept as a pair or small group in a species-only tank; never mix different Parosphromenus species.Max Size: 1.4 inches (3.5 cm)TDS: 0 to 30 (conductivity under 35 µS/cm)pH: 3.5 to 6.0Temperature: 75 to 82°FDiet: Micropredator. Wild-caught fish usually take only live food. We currently feed live baby brine shrimp, daphnia and blackworms.Breeding: Cave spawner with a head-up courtship: the male flutters his fin edges and zig-zags around the female. Clutches are small, about 8 to 25 eggs, laid in narrow caves near the bottom, near the surface or among plants.
