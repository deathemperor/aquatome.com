"Candy"Betta sp. "Candy" is an undescribed mouthbrooder from the unimaculata complex. It probably got its name from its light body speckled with blue and black. This is a very aggressive betta, so we recommend a large tank with lots of hiding spots, and watching a new pair as closely as you can during the first day.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Long and slender with a large, blunt head. Fins: Rounded caudal fin. Males develop longer fins as they mature. Scaling: Scattered blue iridescent scales across the body, giving the speckled look. Coloration: Light body speckled with blue and black. Background: Undescribed species, traded under a trade name; the collection locality has not been published. Care GuideTemperament: Very aggressive. Keep species-only in a large tank with plenty of caves and cover, and watch new pairs closely.Max Size: 3+ inchesTDS: 60 to 100pH: 5.0 to 7.0Temperature: 72 to 82°F (they prefer cooler water, but warmer water brings out color and breeding behavior)Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets.Health: In our experience, fish in this complex are very prone to ich and velvet during the first few weeks after import, so watch new arrivals closely.Breeding: Paternal mouthbrooder. The female collects the eggs during spawning and passes them to the male, who holds them in his mouth for about 2 to 3 weeks. Keep the tank quiet and leave a holding male alone, since a disturbed male may swallow or spit out the brood. He releases fully formed fry that can eat baby brine shrimp right away.
