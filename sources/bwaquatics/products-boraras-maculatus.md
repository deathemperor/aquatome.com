It lives in blackwater peat swamp forests of Peninsular Malaysia, southern Thailand, Singapore and eastern Sumatra; the type locality is Muar, Johor. Our fish are from the Sumatran population, where the water is extremely soft and acidic under dense forest canopy with heavy leaf litter. They make great tankmates for other blackwater fish such as licorice gouramis or small mouthbrooding bettas like Betta channoides and Betta albimarginata. Their best coloration comes in a dimly lit, tannin-stained tank with a school of 10 or more.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Tiny, slightly deeper-bodied than other Boraras. Fins: Red to orange fins with dark markings at the base of the dorsal and anal fins. Coloration: Copper-orange to red body with a large dark spot on the side, a smaller spot above the anal fin and one at the tail base. Habitat: Shaded peat swamp streams and pools with little current. Care GuideTemperament: Exceptionally peaceful. Keep in groups of at least 10 to 12 in a species-only or nano community tank with Boraras brigittae, Sundadanio axelrodi, Pseudomugil luminatus or small Caridina shrimp. Avoid large or active fish. Difficulty is intermediate: easy once the aquarium is stable, but not recommended for beginners because they need soft water to thrive.Max Size: About 1 inTank: Slow or still water, leaf litter, driftwood, fine plants, dim light and a dark substrate.TDS: 0 to 60 ppm (under 100)pH: 4.5 to 6.5Temperature: 72 to 79°FDiet: Micro-predator. Finely crushed flakes, micro pellets and live or frozen baby brine shrimp, cyclops and microworms. Several small feedings a day are ideal.Breeding: Egg scatterer that spawns among fine plants, moss or leaf litter in very soft, acidic water and dim light. Remove adults after spawning.
