This group was supplied as coming from Malinau, a little south of the published range. It is told apart from other unimaculata-complex species by a distinct black spot near the base of the tail and a blue gill plate.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Long, slender, almost worm-like body with a large, blunt head. Fully grown males have larger bodies. Fins: Evenly colored caudal fin without bars. Fully grown males have longer pelvic fins. Scaling: Light blue iridescent scales on the gill plates and lower jaw, with scattered iridescent scales along the body. Coloration: Light brown to tan body with light blue iridescence on the cheeks, lower jaw, body and fins, and a black spot near the tail base. Females have less iridescence and often show two dark lateral bands. Habitat (wild Betta ocellata): Slow-flowing parts of forest streams, ox-bow lakes and isolated pools, among leaf litter, roots and floating leaves. They are excellent jumpers. Care GuideTemperament: Aggressive. Best kept in a species-only tank, since they will eat any fish that fits in their mouth. Give a pair plenty of caves and cover to break line of sight, and keep a tight lid. Recommended 1 pair per 10 gal, or 20 gal for breeding.Max Size: 3 to 4 inchesTDS: 60 to 100pH: 5.0 to 7.0Temperature: 72 to 82°F (they prefer cooler water, but warmer water brings out color and breeding behavior)Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets.Health: In our experience, fish in this complex are very prone to ich and velvet during the first few weeks after import, so watch new arrivals closely.Breeding: Paternal mouthbrooder. The female collects the eggs during spawning and passes them to the male, who holds them in his mouth for about 2 to 3 weeks. Keep the tank quiet and leave a holding male alone, since a disturbed male may swallow or spit out the brood. He releases fully formed fry that can eat baby brine shrimp right away.
