IUCN Red List: N/A (this is a hybrid and does not occur in the wild) Origin: Captive bred Betta Splendens "Plakat Fighter" Grey Betta Splendens "Plakat Fighter" Grey is a hybrid line bred specifically for fighting in Thailand. That history gave it some unique traits: it is extremely hardy, with a heavier bone structure and a compact, muscular build. Plakat fighters usually have no mask on the head, and this line carries a grey coloration. Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Compact, muscular and robust, typically 1.5 to 2 inches (4 to 5 cm), built for agility and strength. Fins: Short, rounded caudal fin made for quick movement and durability, unlike the long, flowing fins of ornamental bettas. Scaling: Grey iridescence across the body scales. Coloration: Grey body and fins. Males color up strongest when displaying. Females are duller. Operculum: Prominent gill covers with iridescent scales and bold markings, flared wide to intimidate rivals. Background: Not found in the wild. Bred in captivity in Thailand for fighting, traditionally kept in small, separate containers in warm (26 to 30°C), neutral water (pH 6.5 to 7.5). Care Guide Temperament: Males are VERY aggressive and should be kept alone or with non-male betta tankmates. Multiple females can be kept together in a planted community tank if there is enough space. Max Size: 2.3 inches TDS: 60 to 200 (we keep them in soft, low-TDS water to match our wild bettas, but they tolerate a wider range) pH: 5.0 to 7.0 Temperature: 75 to 85°F (much better coloration above 78°F). Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets. Breeding: Bubblenester. The male builds a bubble nest under a floating leaf or plants and places the eggs in it after spawning. Remove the female once spawning is done. Eggs hatch in 24 to 48 hours, and the fry are free-swimming about 3 days later. Start them on infusoria, then move to baby brine shrimp. Keep the surface calm so the nest stays intact.
