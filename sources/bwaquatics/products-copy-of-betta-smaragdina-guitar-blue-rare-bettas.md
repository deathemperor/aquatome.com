IUCN Red List: N/A (this is a hybrid color form and does not occur in the wild) Origin: Captive bred Betta Smaragdina Guitar "Blue" Betta Smaragdina Guitar "Blue" is a hybrid color form of the smaragdina guitar, a hardy member of the Betta splendens complex. Smaragdina guitars get their name from the spider-web pattern in the tail fin, and they carry the snake-like scaling on the face and body that smaragdina types are known for. Wild-type guitars are metallic green, but this line has been bred for a deep blue iridescence. Like other smaragdinas, they tolerate cooler water better than most splendens, which makes them a great choice for beginners. Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Slender and elongated, with a streamlined, slightly compressed body typical of the Betta splendens complex. Reaches about 2.3 inches (6 cm), larger than some related species. Tail Shape: Usually rounded or spadetail. Long fins with a spider-web pattern of black transverse bars between the caudal rays, unlike the spotted or plain fins of standard Betta smaragdina. Scaling: Each body scale is outlined, creating a reticulated "snakeskin" pattern from the gill plate to the tail base. The center of each scale carries blue iridescence, so the pattern reads as a blue net over a dark body. The same snake-scale pattern continues onto the operculum. Coloration: Dark brown to black base with iridescent blue scales. Males show the strongest blue, with red accents in the fins. Females are duller, with brownish tones and muted blue iridescence. Operculum: Black with a snake-scale pattern of blue iridescent scales in males, most visible when flaring. Females have fainter, simpler markings. Habitat (wild Smaragdina Guitar): Native to northeastern Thailand, around Bueng Khong Long Lake in Bueng Kan province. Lives in slow-moving, shallow water such as rice paddies, swamps and streams with dense vegetation and low oxygen. Prefers warm (25 to 27°C), slightly acidic (pH 5 to 7) water and uses its labyrinth organ to breathe air. Care Guide Temperament: Males are VERY aggressive and should be kept alone or with non-male betta tankmates. Multiple females can be kept together in a planted community tank if there is enough space. Max Size: 2.3 inches TDS: 60 to 200 (we keep them in soft, low-TDS water to match our wild bettas, but they tolerate a wider range) pH: 5.0 to 7.0 Temperature: 75 to 85°F (much better coloration above 78°F). Known to withstand temperatures as low as 60°F. Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets. Breeding: Bubblenester. The male builds a bubble nest under a floating leaf or plants and places the eggs in it after spawning. Remove the female once spawning is done. Eggs hatch in 24 to 48 hours, and the fry are free-swimming about 3 days later. Start them on infusoria, then move to baby brine shrimp. Keep the surface calm so the nest stays intact.
