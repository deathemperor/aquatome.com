It is a close relative of Betta pallifina. The name means "tattooed", for the distinct black pattern on the body, which is usually strongest in females.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Long and slender. Males have broader heads than females. Fins: Long anal fin and pointed caudal fin. Fully grown males have longer pelvic fins. Scaling: Light blue iridescent scales on the cheeks and lower jaw, with scattered iridescent scales along the body. Coloration: Light brown to tan body with light blue iridescence on the cheeks, lower jaw, body and fins. Females have less iridescence and a stronger black body pattern. Habitat (wild Betta compuncta): Mostly small, slow-flowing streams in swampy heath forest, sometimes larger, faster tributaries. FishBase records very acidic water, pH 3.7 to 5.3. Care GuideTemperament: Aggressive. Best kept in a species-only tank, since they will eat any fish that fits in their mouth. Give a pair plenty of caves and cover to break line of sight. Recommended 1 pair per 10 gal, or 20 gal for breeding.Max Size: 3 inchesTDS: 60 to 100pH: 3.7 to 5.5Temperature: 72 to 82°F (they prefer cooler water, but warmer water brings out color and breeding behavior)Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets.Health: In our experience, fish in this complex are very prone to ich and velvet during the first few weeks after import, so watch new arrivals closely.Breeding: Paternal mouthbrooder. The female collects the eggs during spawning and passes them to the male, who holds them in his mouth for about 12 to 18 days (usually around 14). Keep the tank quiet and leave a holding male alone, since a disturbed male may swallow or spit out the brood. He releases fully formed fry that can eat baby brine shrimp right away.
