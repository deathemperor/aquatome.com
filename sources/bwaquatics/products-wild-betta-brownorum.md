It is named after Barbara and Allan Brown, who first collected it, and its type locality is a small peat swamp near Sibu.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Small and compact, one of the smallest species in the coccina complex. Fins: Males have a longer anal fin and longer unpaired fins than females. Scaling: Small, even scales. The green spot is a cluster of iridescent scales in the middle of the flank. Coloration: Males are intense red with a green iridescent spot on the flank. Spot size varies by locality, and a few fish in this group have a noticeably larger spot than other brownorum we have seen. Females have shorter fins and duller color. Habitat (wild Betta brownorum): Peat swamp forest and blackwater streams with very acidic water; pH as low as 3.0 has been recorded. Care GuideTemperament: Moderately aggressive. Males can be territorial when setting up a breeding site, but because of their small size you can keep multiple pairs with enough space and cover. Expect fin nipping. Recommended 1 pair per 5 gal.Max Size: 1.0 to 1.2 inchesTDS: 60 to 100pH: 3.0 to 6.0Temperature: 75 to 82°FDiet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets.Breeding: Bubblenester. The male builds a small bubble nest at the surface, usually under a floating leaf, inside a floating cave or among plants, and places the eggs in it after spawning. Pairs can often stay together in a well-planted tank; remove the female if the male keeps chasing her. Eggs hatch in 24 to 48 hours, and the fry are free-swimming about 3 days later.
