IUCN Red List: N/A (this is a hybrid and does not occur in the wild) Origin: Captive bred Alien Betta "Copper" Alien Betta "Copper" is a captive-bred hybrid of several wild species in the Betta splendens complex, from our breeders in Vietnam. Aliens got their name from their unusual head shape, and they are known for a full facial mask, iridescence that spreads over the body and fins, and webbing in the caudal fin. Copper carries the metallic gene over steel blue iridescence, and like all copper bettas, it breeds true. Copper is one of the most unique betta colors, showing hints of different colors as the light angle changes. Genes for iridescence: blbl (steel blue) Genes for metallic: ++ Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Slender and slightly compressed, typical of the Betta splendens complex. Reaches about 2.3 inches (6 cm). Fins: Rounded to spadetail, with webbing between the caudal rays. Iridescence carries out into the fins. Scaling: Dense metallic copper spreads across the scales from head to tail, so the body color looks solid. Coloration: Metallic copper over a dark base, showing hints of other colors as the light angle changes. Males are the most intense. Females are duller. Operculum: Full facial mask. Iridescence covers the face and gill plates, which is one of the main alien traits. Background: Aliens are a line bred in captivity from crosses of wild splendens-complex species and do not occur in the wild. Pairs that "we pick" are not lower quality than the males in the photos. We don't always have time to photograph every fish, but we send our best. Care Guide Temperament: Males are VERY aggressive and should be kept alone or with non-male betta tankmates. Multiple females can be kept together in a planted community tank if there is enough space. Max Size: 2.3 inches TDS: 60 to 200 (we keep them in soft, low-TDS water to match our wild bettas, but they tolerate a wider range) pH: 5.0 to 7.0 Temperature: 75 to 85°F (much better coloration above 78°F). Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets. Breeding: Bubblenester. The male builds a bubble nest under a floating leaf or plants and places the eggs in it after spawning. Remove the female once spawning is done. Eggs hatch in 24 to 48 hours, and the fry are free-swimming about 3 days later. Start them on infusoria, then move to baby brine shrimp. Keep the surface calm so the nest stays intact.
