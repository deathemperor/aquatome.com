Most of its range is in southern and western Sarawak, Malaysia, and this group comes from the handful of populations across the border in West Kalimantan, Indonesia. It is named after Barbara and Allan Brown, who first collected it.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Small and compact, one of the smallest species in the coccina complex. Fins: Males have a longer anal fin and longer unpaired fins than females. Scaling: Small, even scales. The green spot is a cluster of iridescent scales in the middle of the flank. Coloration: Males are intense red with a green iridescent spot on the flank; spot size varies between fish. Females have shorter fins and duller color. Habitat (wild Betta brownorum): Peat swamp forest and blackwater streams with very acidic water; pH as low as 3.0 has been recorded. Care GuideTemperament: Moderately aggressive. Males can be territorial when setting up a breeding site, but because of their small size you can keep multiple pairs with enough space and cover. Expect fin nipping. Recommended 1 pair per 5 gal.Max Size: 1.0 to 1.2 inchesTDS: 60 to 100pH: 3.0 to 6.0Temperature: 75 to 82°FDiet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets.Breeding: Bubblenester. The male builds a small bubble nest at the surface, usually under a floating leaf, inside a floating cave or among plants, and places the eggs in it after spawning. Pairs can often stay together in a well-planted tank; remove the female if the male keeps chasing her. Eggs hatch in 24 to 48 hours, and the fry are free-swimming about 3 days later.
