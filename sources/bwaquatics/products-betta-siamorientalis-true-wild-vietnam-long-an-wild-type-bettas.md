IUCN Red List: N/A Origin: Wild caught (Long An, Vietnam) Betta Siamorientalis "True Wild" Long An, Vietnam Betta Siamorientalis "True Wild" are wild-caught Betta siamorientalis from Long An, Vietnam. This species closely resembles Betta imbellis but has a darker body and head, which is why it is sometimes called the "black imbellis." It lacks the facial iridescence of imbellis, giving it a classic black head. Siamorientalis live in still-water marshes and ditches and prefer warmer water. Like all wild bettas, their natural coloration is metallic green. Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Slender and elongated, with a slightly compressed body. One of the smaller species in the splendens complex, reaching about 1.4 inches (3.6 cm) standard length. Tail Shape: Short, rounded caudal fin, often with a red crescent-shaped band and a thin black edge. Scaling: More black scales than iridescent ones, with scattered blue-green iridescent scales across a dark body. Coloration: Dark brown to black body and head. Males show vivid red on the caudal, anal and pelvic fins with blue or green iridescent accents. Females are duller and brownish with muted red or silvery markings. Operculum: Black with two parallel reddish vertical bars in males, sometimes pale red, greenish-silver or silver depending on the population. Females have fainter markings. Habitat (wild Betta siamorientalis): Native to eastern Thailand, western Cambodia and parts of Vietnam. Lives in freshwater marshes, rice paddies, ponds and canals with dense vegetation and low oxygen, in warm, near-neutral water (pH 6.8 to 7.4). Uses its labyrinth organ to breathe air. Care Guide Temperament: Males are VERY aggressive and should be kept alone or with non-male betta tankmates. Multiple females can be kept together in a planted community tank if there is enough space. Max Size: 2.3 inches TDS: 60 to 200 (we keep them in soft, low-TDS water to match our wild bettas, but they tolerate a wider range) pH: 5.0 to 7.0 Temperature: 75 to 85°F (much better coloration above 78°F). Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets. Breeding: Bubblenester. The male builds a bubble nest under a floating leaf or plants and places the eggs in it after spawning. Remove the female once spawning is done. Eggs hatch in 24 to 48 hours, and the fry are free-swimming about 3 days later. Start them on infusoria, then move to baby brine shrimp. Keep the surface calm so the nest stays intact.
