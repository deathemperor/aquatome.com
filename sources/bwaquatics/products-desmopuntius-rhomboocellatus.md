It lives in slow, heavily tannin-stained peat swamp streams under forest canopy with deep leaf litter. Its name comes from the dark, ring-shaped rhomboid markings on a warm orange-red body that give it a snakeskin look. Groups kept in stable, mature blackwater show the strongest color.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Deep, compressed barb body. Fins: Red-orange fins in adults. Coloration: Orange-red to golden body covered in dark rhomboid rings in an irregular, reticulated pattern. Habitat: Slow, shaded peat swamp streams with leaf litter. Care GuideTemperament: Peaceful schooling fish. Keep in groups of 6 to 12 or more; small groups become shy and lose color. Good with Boraras, Sundadanio, small rasboras and peaceful loaches. Avoid fin-nippers and fast, boisterous fish.Max Size: Usually 2 to 2.4 in; up to about 3.5 in total lengthTank: Leaf litter, botanicals, driftwood and dim light. Gentle current is fine.TDS: Under 120 ppmpH: 4.5 to 6.5Temperature: 75 to 82°FDiet: Prefers small invertebrates. Live or frozen daphnia, brine shrimp, bloodworms and mosquito larvae, plus micro pellets, soft granules and small flakes.Health: Wild fish can be sensitive during acclimation. Avoid sudden changes in pH or hardness and give plenty of cover.Breeding: Egg scatterer with no parental care. Adults eat eggs, so use a separate tank with mesh or marbles on the bottom and fine plants. Eggs hatch in about 24 to 36 hours; feed infusoria first, then baby brine shrimp.
