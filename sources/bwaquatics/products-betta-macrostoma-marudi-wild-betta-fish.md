Betta macrostoma is one of the most sought-after wild bettas for its size and color. Imagine albimarginata or channoides on steroids: males are a rich red with a bold eye-spot in the dorsal fin and banding in the tail. In the wild it lives in shady rainforest streams in northern Borneo. It is more sensitive to water conditions than other large bettas, and its beauty, rarity and difficulty all add to the higher cost.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Long and stocky with a large head and big mouth (macrostoma means "large mouth"). Fins: Males have a dark eye-spot (ocellus) in the dorsal fin and banding in the caudal fin. Scaling: Unlike most of the complex, the gill plate has no iridescent scales; instead males have a red patch there. Coloration: Males are red with a red gill-plate patch and a dorsal eye-spot. Females are less colorful. Habitat (wild Betta macrostoma): Still, shady pools above rainforest waterfalls and quiet stretches of streams, with very soft water measured at pH 4.4 to 5.7. Care GuideTemperament: Aggressive. Best kept in a species-only tank, since they will eat any fish that fits in their mouth. Males fight hard, and the weaker fish in a random pairing can be killed, so watch new pairs closely and give plenty of cover. Recommended 1 pair per 10 gal, or 20 gal for breeding.Max Size: 4 to 4.5 inchesTDS: 60 to 100pH: 4.0 to 6.0Temperature: 68 to 77°F (cooler than most bettas)Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets.Health: In our experience, fish in this complex are very prone to ich and velvet during the first few weeks after import, so watch new arrivals closely. Newly imported macrostoma are also prone to fungal infections.Breeding: Paternal mouthbrooder. The male holds the eggs in his mouth for anywhere from 14 to 35 days before releasing free-swimming fry. Macrostoma males are prone to swallowing the brood if disturbed, so keep the tank very quiet. Fry need humid air above the water for the labyrinth organ to develop properly; don't overfeed them.
