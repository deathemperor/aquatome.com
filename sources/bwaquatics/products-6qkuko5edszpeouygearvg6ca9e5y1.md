Muara Teweh is its type locality. The name means "pale border", for the clear band near the edge of the female's anal and caudal fins.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Long and slender with a large, blunt head. Fully grown males have a larger body. Fins: Lance-shaped caudal fin in adults. Females have a clear band near the edge of the anal and caudal fins. Fully grown males have longer pelvic fins. Scaling: Adult males carry metallic scales on the gill plates that look sky blue to gold depending on the light. Coloration: Brown body with bronze-copper to blue iridescence on the cheeks, body and fins. Females have less iridescence. Habitat (wild Betta pallifina): Clear headwater forest streams, with pH recorded between 6.6 and 7.4. Care GuideTemperament: Aggressive. Best kept in a species-only tank, since they will eat any fish that fits in their mouth. Give a pair plenty of caves and cover to break line of sight. Recommended 1 pair per 10 gal, or 20 gal for breeding.Max Size: 3 to 3.5 inchesTDS: 60 to 100pH: 6.5 to 7.5Temperature: 72 to 82°F (they prefer cooler water, but warmer water brings out color and breeding behavior)Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets.Health: In our experience, fish in this complex are very prone to ich and velvet during the first few weeks after import, so watch new arrivals closely.Breeding: Paternal mouthbrooder and easy to breed in captivity. The female collects the eggs during spawning and passes them to the male, who holds them in his mouth for up to 3 weeks. Keep the tank quiet and leave a holding male alone, since a disturbed male may swallow or spit out the brood. He releases fully formed fry that can eat baby brine shrimp right away.
