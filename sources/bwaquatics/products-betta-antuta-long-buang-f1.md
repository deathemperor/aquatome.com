"Antuta" Long Bang, young F1Betta sp. "Antuta" is an undescribed member of the unimaculata complex from Bulungan Regency in North Kalimantan, Indonesian Borneo. Unlike most bettas, it lives in clear, flowing highland streams rather than swamps. It is best known for the large, dragon-like iridescent scales on the head.These are first-generation captive bred (F1) young adults from the Long Bang line. Most have a half mask, and their color will keep improving as they mature.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Long and slender with a large, blunt head. Males grow larger and develop a broader head. Fins: Rounded caudal fin. Males develop longer fins as they mature. Scaling: Large, dragon-like iridescent scales on the head and gill plates. On young fish the mask is still filling in. Coloration: Brown to bronze body with blue-green iridescence on the head and body. Females are browner, with an orange to brown glow on the belly. Habitat (wild Betta sp. Antuta): Clear, well-oxygenated highland streams in Bulungan. Care GuideTemperament: Aggressive. Best kept in a species-only tank, since they will eat any fish that fits in their mouth. Give a pair plenty of caves and cover to break line of sight. Recommended 1 pair per 10 gal, or 20 gal for breeding.Max Size: 4 to 5 inchesTDS: 60 to 100pH: 5.5 to 7.5Temperature: 72 to 82°F (they prefer cooler water, but warmer water brings out color and breeding behavior)Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets.Health: In our experience, fish in this complex are very prone to ich and velvet during the first few weeks after import, so watch new arrivals closely.Breeding: Paternal mouthbrooder. The female collects the eggs during spawning and passes them to the male, who holds them in his mouth for about 2 to 3 weeks. Keep the tank quiet and leave a holding male alone, since a disturbed male may swallow or spit out the brood. He releases fully formed fry that can eat baby brine shrimp right away.
