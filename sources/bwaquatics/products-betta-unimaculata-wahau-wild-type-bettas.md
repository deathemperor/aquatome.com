It is a mouthbrooder from eastern Borneo, and many East Kalimantan localities are sold as unimaculata or unimaculata types. The Wahau form is one of the larger ones, with males normally reaching over 3.5 inches.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Long and slender with a large, blunt head. Adult males grow larger than females. Fins: Rounded caudal fin. Fully grown males have longer pelvic fins. Scaling: Light blue iridescent scales on the cheeks and lower jaw, with scattered iridescent scales along the body. Coloration: Light brown to tan body with light blue iridescence on the cheeks, lower jaw, body and fins. Females have less iridescence. Habitat (wild Betta unimaculata): Clear forest streams with flowing water; FishBase records pH 6.5 to 7.5 and 70 to 77°F. Care GuideTemperament: Aggressive. Best kept in a species-only tank, since they will eat any fish that fits in their mouth. Give a pair plenty of caves and cover to break line of sight. Recommended 1 pair per 10 gal, or 20 gal for breeding.Max Size: 4+ inchesTDS: 60 to 100pH: 6.5 to 7.5Temperature: 72 to 82°F (they prefer cooler water, but warmer water brings out color and breeding behavior)Diet: Carnivorous. The female collects the eggs during spawning and passes them to the male, who holds them in his mouth for about 2 to 3 weeks.
