It is a large mouthbrooder known for the dark vertical bars on the lower body. Color varies by locality, and this Tanah Grogot form has a more reddish body than the normal type.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Long and slender with a large, blunt head. Fins: Lance-shaped caudal fin with no bars on the dorsal, anal or caudal fins. Fully grown males have longer pelvic fins. Scaling: Blue iridescent scales on the cheeks and gill plates, with scattered iridescent scales on the body. Coloration: Reddish-brown body with blue iridescence on the cheeks, body and fins, and 10 to 14 dark vertical bars on the lower body that show more at some times than others. Females have less iridescence. Habitat (wild Betta patoti): Forest streams in southern East Kalimantan, from the Balikpapan area north into Kutai Kartanegara. Care GuideTemperament: Aggressive. Best kept in a species-only tank, since they will eat any fish that fits in their mouth. Give a pair plenty of caves and cover to break line of sight. Recommended 1 pair per 10 gal, or 20 gal for breeding.Max Size: 4.2 inchesTDS: 60 to 100pH: 4.0 to 7.0Temperature: 72 to 82°F (they prefer cooler water, but warmer water brings out color and breeding behavior)Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets.Health: In our experience, fish in this complex are very prone to ich and velvet during the first few weeks after import, so watch new arrivals closely.Breeding: Paternal mouthbrooder. The female collects the eggs during spawning and passes them to the male, who holds them in his mouth for about 2 to 3 weeks. Keep the tank quiet and leave a holding male alone, since a disturbed male may swallow or spit out the brood. He releases fully formed fry that can eat baby brine shrimp right away.
