It looks a lot like pallifina: compared with other unimaculata, its body is long and slender but much smaller overall. All wild-caught fish go through a month of quarantine to make sure they are free of disease. They do well in a species-only tank and are a good fish for beginners getting into mouthbrooders.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Long and slender, smaller than most unimaculata. Fins: Blue-teal fins with a metallic copper coating in both sexes. Scaling: Iridescent scales on the cheeks and along the flanks that look blue to teal depending on the light. Coloration: Brown body with blue to teal iridescence on the cheeks and flanks. Females have less iridescence. Habitat (wild Betta unimaculata): Clear forest streams with flowing water; FishBase records pH 6.5 to 7.5 and 70 to 77°F. Care GuideTemperament: Aggressive. Best kept in a species-only tank, since they will eat any fish that fits in their mouth. Give a pair plenty of caves and cover to break line of sight. Recommended 1 pair per 10 gal, or 20 gal for breeding.Max Size: 3+ inchesTDS: 60 to 250 (very adaptable)pH: 6.5 to 7.5Temperature: 72 to 82°F (they prefer cooler water, but warmer water brings out color and breeding behavior)Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets.Health: In our experience, fish in this complex are very prone to ich and velvet during the first few weeks after import, so watch new arrivals closely.Breeding: Paternal mouthbrooder. The female collects the eggs during spawning and passes them to the male, who holds them in his mouth for about 2 to 3 weeks. Keep the tank quiet and leave a holding male alone, since a disturbed male may swallow or spit out the brood. He releases fully formed fry that can eat baby brine shrimp right away.
