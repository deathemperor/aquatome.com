All wild-caught fish go through a month of quarantine to make sure they are free of disease, and they are a good fish for beginners getting into mouthbrooders.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Long and slender with a large, blunt head. Fins: Pointed caudal fin. Both sexes have blue-teal fins with a metallic copper coating and red banding. Scaling: Metallic scales on the gill plates that look blue to gold depending on the light. Coloration: Brown body with blue-teal iridescence and red on the fins. Females have less iridescence. Background: Traded under the Mahakam locality; not yet matched to a confirmed wild population of pallifina. Care GuideTemperament: Aggressive. Best kept in a species-only tank, since they will eat any fish that fits in their mouth. Give a pair plenty of caves and cover to break line of sight. Recommended 1 pair per 10 gal, or 20 gal for breeding.Max Size: 3+ inchesTDS: 60 to 250 (very adaptable)pH: 6.5 to 7.5Temperature: 72 to 82°F (they prefer cooler water, but warmer water brings out color and breeding behavior)Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets.Health: In our experience, fish in this complex are very prone to ich and velvet during the first few weeks after import, so watch new arrivals closely.Breeding: Paternal mouthbrooder. The female collects the eggs during spawning and passes them to the male, who holds them in his mouth for about 2 to 3 weeks. Keep the tank quiet and leave a holding male alone, since a disturbed male may swallow or spit out the brood. He releases fully formed fry that can eat baby brine shrimp right away.
