IUCN Red List: N/A (this is a captive color form; wild Betta imbellis is listed as Least Concern) Origin: Captive bred Betta Imbellis "Royal Blue" Betta Imbellis "Royal Blue" is a captive color form of Betta imbellis, the crescent betta, a peaceful-looking but aggressive member of the Betta splendens complex. Wild imbellis are metallic green, but royal blue lacks the metallic layer, so only the blue iridescence underneath shows. Imbellis are known for the red crescent at the edge of the tail and the red teardrop on the anal fin. Genes for iridescence: Blbl (royal blue) Genes for metallic: -- Royal blue does not breed true, so these are not often sold as pairs. A pair will produce a mix of turquoise, steel blue and royal blue offspring. Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Slender and elongated, with a slightly compressed body. Reaches about 2.3 inches (6 cm). Tail Shape: Short, rounded caudal fin with a red crescent-shaped band along the edge. Red on the ventral and anal fins, including the red teardrop on the anal fin. Scaling: Royal blue iridescent scales spread across a dark brown body, with no metallic sheen. Coloration: Dark brown to black base with deep royal blue iridescence and red fin accents. Males are the most intense. Females are duller and brownish. Operculum: Two iridescent blue vertical bars on the gill covers in males. Females have faint markings. Habitat (wild Betta imbellis): Native to southern Thailand, Peninsular Malaysia and northern Sumatra, with introduced populations in Singapore. Lives in rice paddies, swamps, roadside ditches and ponds with dense vegetation and leaf litter, in warm, slightly acidic to neutral water (pH 4 to 7). Uses its labyrinth organ to breathe air. Care Guide Temperament: Males are VERY aggressive and should be kept alone or with non-male betta tankmates. Multiple females can be kept together in a planted community tank if there is enough space. Max Size: 2.3 inches TDS: 60 to 200 (we keep them in soft, low-TDS water to match our wild bettas, but they tolerate a wider range) pH: 6.5 to 7.5 Temperature: 75 to 85°F (much better coloration above 78°F). Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets. Breeding: Bubblenester. The male builds a bubble nest under a floating leaf or plants and places the eggs in it after spawning. Remove the female once spawning is done. Eggs hatch in 24 to 48 hours, and the fry are free-swimming about 3 days later. Start them on infusoria, then move to baby brine shrimp. Keep the surface calm so the nest stays intact.
