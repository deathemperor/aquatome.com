It lives in slow, heavily shaded blackwater peat swamp streams and pools with submerged roots, branches and decomposing leaf litter. Mature males develop an intense crimson-red body crossed by a bold dark stripe; females are rounder and less colorful. These are captive-bred young adults and may arrive lightly colored after transport. Their red will strengthen with maturity, good food and stable water.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Tiny, slender rasbora. Fins: Red and black markings on the dorsal and anal fins in males. Coloration: Crimson-red body with a dark stripe along the side, a red stripe above it and a red spot at the tail base. Habitat: Shaded peat swamp streams and pools. Care GuideTemperament: Peaceful schooling fish. Keep in groups of 10 to 20 or more. Good with other Boraras, Sundadanio, small Parosphromenus, sparkling gouramis, pygmy corydoras and dwarf shrimp. Avoid large, aggressive or fast-feeding fish. A species-only tank or a community of similarly sized nano fish is ideal.Max Size: About 0.8 in standard lengthTank: Gentle filtration, leaf litter, botanicals, driftwood, floating plants and dim light. Feed small portions once or twice daily and make sure shy fish eat.Breeding: Continuous egg scatterer with no parental care. Eggs are laid a few at a time in Java moss or fine plants. Adults eat eggs and fry, so use a separate dim tank with very soft, acidic water and moss or mesh.
