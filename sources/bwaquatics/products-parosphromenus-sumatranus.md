It was described in 1955 and is endemic to Sumatra, mainly the Jambi area. It was treated as a subspecies of Parosphromenus deissneri until 2005, and it is still often sold as "deissneri", "red line" or "fire red". It mixes the fin colors of the common licorice gouramis with the head-up courtship of the slim ornaticauda and parvulus group.Learn more from our friends at the Parosphromenus Project.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Two forms occur, one with a higher back and one leaner. Fins: Light red bands in the dorsal and anal fins, and a round black spot at the rear of the dorsal-fin base. The caudal fin is plain pale red to black-red, sometimes with a very short filament. The pelvic filament is black. Coloration: Brown body with a narrow dark band where the body meets the anal fin. Females have the same pattern with a fainter dorsal spot. Similar Species: The black dorsal spot separates it from Parosphromenus bintan and P. deissneri, and its black pelvic filament separates it from bintan, which has a white one. Habitat (wild Parosphromenus sumatranus): Blackwater streams and swamps in Jambi and neighboring basins in Sumatra. Care GuideTemperament: Peaceful and very shy; give them plenty of hiding places. Keep a pair in 5 gal, or a group of 3 to 6 in 10 gal with caves and leaf litter. Never mix different Parosphromenus species.Max Size: 1.4 inches (3.5 cm)TDS: 0 to 50pH: 4.0 to 6.0Temperature: 72 to 82°FDiet: Micropredator. They only eat live food. We currently feed live baby brine shrimp, daphnia and blackworms.Breeding: Cave spawner with a head-up courtship, sometimes in a tipped-over position.
