It is one of the larger licorice gouramis and is named for the filament on the male's tail. The Parosphromenus Project calls it one of the least specialized species in the genus and a good choice for less experienced keepers, though like all paros it needs live food.Learn more from our friends at the Parosphromenus Project.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Slender, with a particularly long-based dorsal fin. Fins: The male's pointed caudal fin ends in a filament and has a brown-red center, a broad black frame and a thin white to blue edge. The dorsal and anal fins repeat this pattern in brown-red or blue-green. Pelvic fins are green-blue with long filaments. Coloration: Bronze body tones. Courting males can turn very dark overall. Females are pale, with a pointed tail and only a faint filament. Habitat (wild Parosphromenus filamentosus): Peat swamp forest and blackwater streams near Banjarmasin, an area where much of the forest has been cleared. Care GuideTemperament: Peaceful and shy. Keep a pair in 5 gal, or a group of 3 to 6 in 10 gal with plenty of caves and leaf litter. They can share a tank with tiny, calm blackwater fish such as Boraras. Never mix different Parosphromenus species.Max Size: 1.8 inches (4.5 cm)TDS: 0 to 50pH: 4.0 to 6.0Temperature: 72 to 82°FDiet: Micropredator. They only eat live food. We currently feed live baby brine shrimp, daphnia and blackworms.Breeding: Cave spawner with a head-down courtship. The male builds probably the most distinct foam nest in the genus, carrying air down from the surface several times. Clutches are usually 50 to 70 eggs and can reach about 200.
