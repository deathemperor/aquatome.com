IUCN Red List: Data Deficient (not enough information at this time) Origin: Captive bred Betta Smaragdina "Buriram" Betta Smaragdina "Buriram" is a regional form of Betta smaragdina from Buriram province in northeastern Thailand. Buriram smaragdina are known for their extra thick iridescence, which gives them more color coverage than other populations. Like all smaragdina, they come from a harsher environment than most splendens and handle lower temperatures well, which makes them a great choice for beginners. Bred by a breeder in Vietnam, imported in July, around 7 months old. Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Slender and elongated, with a streamlined, slightly compressed body. One of the larger species in the splendens complex, reaching about 2.3 inches (6 cm). Tail Shape: Usually spadetail, sometimes roundtail. The caudal fin has transverse bars and lacks the red crescent-shaped band seen in some other bettas. Fins are often longer than in other wild splendens types. Scaling: Each body scale is outlined, with dense green-blue iridescence covering more of each scale than in other populations. The same snake-scale pattern continues onto the operculum. Coloration: Dark brown to black base with heavy green and blue iridescence across the body and fins. Males are the most intense, with red accents in the fins. Females are duller and brownish. Operculum: Snake-scale pattern of iridescent green or blue scales, with two parallel red or silvery vertical bars in males, brightest when flaring. Females have simpler, fainter markings. Habitat (wild Betta smaragdina): Native to northeastern Thailand, Laos and parts of Cambodia in the Mekong Basin. Lives in slow-moving, shallow water such as rice paddies, ponds, marshes and streams with dense vegetation. Prefers warm (24 to 30°C), slightly acidic to neutral (pH 5.5 to 7.5) water and uses its labyrinth organ to breathe air. Care Guide Temperament: Males are VERY aggressive and should be kept alone or with non-male betta tankmates. Multiple females can be kept together in a planted community tank if there is enough space. Max Size: 2.3 inches TDS: 60 to 200 (we keep them in soft, low-TDS water to match our wild bettas, but they tolerate a wider range) pH: 5.0 to 7.0 Temperature: 75 to 85°F (much better coloration above 78°F). Known to withstand temperatures as low as 60°F. Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets. Breeding: Bubblenester. The male builds a bubble nest under a floating leaf or plants and places the eggs in it after spawning. Remove the female once spawning is done. Eggs hatch in 24 to 48 hours, and the fry are free-swimming about 3 days later. Start them on infusoria, then move to baby brine shrimp. Keep the surface calm so the nest stays intact.
