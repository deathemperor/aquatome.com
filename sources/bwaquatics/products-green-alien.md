IUCN Red List: N/A (this is a hybrid and does not occur in the wild) Origin: Captive bred Alien Betta "Green" Alien Betta "Green" is a captive-bred hybrid of several wild species in the Betta splendens complex, from our breeder in Vietnam. Aliens got their name from their unusual head shape, and they are known for a full facial mask, iridescence that spreads over the body and fins, and webbing in the caudal fin. Green carries the metallic gene over turquoise iridescence, so the color shifts with the angle of the light. Sometimes called teal. Genes for iridescence: BlBl (turquoise) Genes for metallic: ++ "Grass green" aliens carry the same double metallic over royal blue iridescence (Blbl). Both are sold as green. The easiest way to tell green from teal is that green always has metallic, so it changes color as the fish turns in the light. Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Slender and slightly compressed, typical of the Betta splendens complex. Reaches about 2.3 inches (6 cm). Tail Shape: Rounded to spadetail, with webbing between the caudal rays. Iridescence carries out into the fins. Scaling: Dense metallic green iridescence spreads across the scales from head to tail, so the body color looks solid. Coloration: Metallic green to teal over a dark base, shifting with the light angle. Males are the most intense. Females are duller. Operculum: Full facial mask. Iridescence covers the face and gill plates, which is one of the main alien traits. Background: Aliens are a line bred in captivity from crosses of wild splendens-complex species and do not occur in the wild. Care Guide Temperament: Males are VERY aggressive and should be kept alone or with non-male betta tankmates. Multiple females can be kept together in a planted community tank if there is enough space. Max Size: 2.3 inches TDS: 60 to 200 (we keep them in soft, low-TDS water to match our wild bettas, but they tolerate a wider range) pH: 5.0 to 7.0 Temperature: 75 to 85°F (much better coloration above 78°F). Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets. Breeding: Bubblenester. The male builds a bubble nest under a floating leaf or plants and places the eggs in it after spawning. Remove the female once spawning is done. Eggs hatch in 24 to 48 hours, and the fry are free-swimming about 3 days later. Start them on infusoria, then move to baby brine shrimp. Keep the surface calm so the nest stays intact.
