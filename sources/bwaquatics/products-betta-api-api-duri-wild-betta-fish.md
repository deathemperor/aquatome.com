"Api Api Duri"Betta sp. "Api Api" is an undescribed member of the coccina complex from the peat swamps of Sumatra, Indonesia. This group comes from Duri in Riau province. It is one of the smallest red bettas in the hobby, and males show an intense red body with reflective blue eyes.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Small and slender. Fins: Rounded caudal fin that comes to a slight point. Males in breeding condition show pale to white edging on the dorsal and caudal fins. Scaling: Small, even scales with a uniform red tone. Some males show a faint metallic sheen on the flank scales under direct light. Coloration: Males are intense red with metallic blue eyes. Females are smaller and duller red to brown. Habitat (wild Betta sp. Api Api): Shaded, tannin-stained forest pools and slow streams with deep leaf litter and fallen branches. Care GuideTemperament: Moderately aggressive. Males can be territorial when setting up a breeding site, but because of their small size you can keep multiple pairs with enough space and cover. Expect fin nipping. Recommended 1 pair per 5 gal.Max Size: 1.2 to 1.5 inchesTDS: 60 to 100pH: 4.0 to 6.0Temperature: 75 to 82°FDiet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets.Breeding: Bubblenester. The male builds a small bubble nest at the surface, usually under a floating leaf, inside a floating cave or among plants, and places the eggs in it after spawning. Pairs can often stay together in a well-planted tank; remove the female if the male keeps chasing her. Eggs hatch in 24 to 48 hours, and the fry are free-swimming about 3 days later. Start them on infusoria, then move to baby brine shrimp. Keep the surface calm so the nest stays intact. Share Link View full details
