"Jade"Betta sp. "Jade" is an undescribed coccina-type betta from Sumatra. Collectors have known it for some time, but it has only recently been photographed and documented. It looks like a mix of Betta persephone or miniopinna and Betta sp. "Api Api": a bright red body like Api Api with a green-blue iridescent patch on the rear of the flank.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Small and slender. Fins: Narrow, tall pelvic fins with a blue-white iridescent rim like Api Api. The caudal fin is rounded and comes to a point, and the anal fin is longer than that of persephone but shorter than that of Api Api. Scaling: Small, even scales. On males, the scales from the start of the anal fin back to the caudal fin carry green-blue iridescence, forming the jade patch. Coloration: Males are bright red with the jade patch on the rear flank. Females look very similar to female persephone or miniopinna. Habitat (wild Betta sp. Jade): Blackwater peat swamps in Sumatra. A true blackwater fish that needs very soft, acidic water and is not suited to beginners. Care GuideTemperament: Moderately aggressive. Males can be territorial when setting up a breeding site, but because of their small size you can keep multiple pairs with enough space and cover. Expect fin nipping. Recommended 1 pair per 5 gal.Max Size: 1.2 to 1.5 inchesTDS: 60 to 100pH: 4.0 to 6.0Temperature: 75 to 82°FDiet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live baby brine shrimp, live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets.Breeding: Bubblenester. The male builds a small bubble nest at the surface, usually under a floating leaf, inside a floating cave or among plants, and places the eggs in it after spawning. Pairs can often stay together in a well-planted tank; remove the female if the male keeps chasing her. Eggs hatch in 24 to 48 hours, and the fry are free-swimming about 3 days later.
