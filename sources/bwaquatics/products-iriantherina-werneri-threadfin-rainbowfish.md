They have several long flowing fins on top and bottom that resemble threads, which gives them their name. The males get beautiful colors when dominant or breeding, and they are a must-see when showing off to each other.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Small, slender, slightly compressed body. Fins: Males have a tall, feathery first dorsal fin, and second dorsal and anal fins with long thread-like extensions often longer than the body. Lyre-shaped tail with red edges. Coloration: Silver to gold body with black and red fin markings. Females are plain silver with short fins. Habitat: Densely vegetated, slow or still water. Care GuideTemperament: Peaceful and slow-swimming. Keep in groups with more females than males. Do not keep with fin-nippers, as the long fins are easy targets.Max Size: About 1.6 in standard length; 1.5 to 2 in including finspH: Currently kept at a pH of 6.0 to 7.0Temperature: Currently kept at 72 to 77°FDiet: They are currently feeding on crushed pellets, baby brine shrimp and black worms. Their small mouths need small foods.Breeding: Continuous spawner. Eggs are attached to fine plants, moss or spawning mops over several days.
