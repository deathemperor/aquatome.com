Horst Linke found it near Sukamara, and it also occurs around Pangkalanbuun, sometimes in the same waters as Parosphromenus opallios. It is easy to recognize by the big black spots on its sides and the long filament on the male's tail. The Parosphromenus Project describes it as robust and less sensitive than most licorice gouramis, which makes it one of the better choices for a first paro.Learn more from our friends at the Parosphromenus Project.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Slender. Males are usually larger than females. Fins: Spotted dorsal, anal and caudal fins in both sexes. Males have a much longer dorsal fin, a larger caudal fin with a filament up to about 0.6 inches, and long pelvic filaments that often curve in older fish. Coloration: Light beige to bronze or burgundy body with 0 to 3 (usually 2) large black spots on each side. Some fish have up to 15 small red dots around the black spots. Similar Species: Parosphromenus pahuensis looks similar but has a round tail with no filament and no red dots on the sides. Habitat (wild Parosphromenus linkei): Shaded peat swamp pools and slow blackwater streams in the southern lowlands of Central Kalimantan. Care GuideTemperament: Peaceful and shy. Keep a pair in 5 gal, or a group of 3 to 6 in 10 gal with plenty of caves and leaf litter. They can share a tank with tiny, calm blackwater fish such as Boraras. Never mix different Parosphromenus species, since they can hybridize.Max Size: 1.8 inches (4.5 cm), up to 2 inches including the tail filamentTDS: 0 to 50pH: 4.0 to 6.0Temperature: 72 to 82°FDiet: Micropredator. Wild-caught fish usually take only live food. We currently feed live baby brine shrimp, daphnia and blackworms.Breeding: Cave spawner with a head-down courtship. The male sometimes builds a very large bubble nest on the cave ceiling, and large females can lay 100 or more eggs. The male guards the eggs and larvae until the fry swim free.
