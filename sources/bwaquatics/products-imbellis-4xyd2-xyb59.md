IUCN Red List: N/A (wild Betta splendens is Vulnerable, but this variety does not occur in the wild) Origin: Captive bred Betta Splendens "Red Spade Tail" Betta Splendens "Red Spade Tail" is a domestic Betta splendens line from our breeder in Vietnam. This line has a red spade tail and a Cambodian body, meaning a solid pale body with red fins, with metallic teal iridescence. All males come from the same lineage and are generally similar. Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Slender and slightly compressed, typical of Betta splendens. Reaches about 2.3 inches (6 cm). Tail Shape: Spadetail, with the caudal fin coming to a point. Solid red fins. Scaling: Metallic teal iridescence over a pale Cambodian body. Coloration: Pale body with red fins and metallic teal iridescence. Males are the most intense. Females are duller. Operculum: Teal iridescence on the gill covers, brightest when flaring. Background: A domesticated form of Betta splendens, line bred in captivity. Does not occur in the wild. Care Guide Temperament: Males are VERY aggressive and should be kept alone or with non-male betta tankmates. Multiple females can be kept together in a planted community tank if there is enough space. Max Size: 2.3 inches TDS: 60 to 200 (we keep them in soft, low-TDS water to match our wild bettas, but they tolerate a wider range) pH: 5.0 to 7.0 Temperature: 75 to 85°F (much better coloration above 78°F). Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets. Breeding: Bubblenester. The male builds a bubble nest under a floating leaf or plants and places the eggs in it after spawning. Remove the female once spawning is done. Eggs hatch in 24 to 48 hours, and the fry are free-swimming about 3 days later. Start them on infusoria, then move to baby brine shrimp. Keep the surface calm so the nest stays intact.
