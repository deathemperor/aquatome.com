IUCN Red List: Data Deficient (not enough information at this time, but most likely highly endangered in the wild) Origin: Captive bred from wild-caught stock Betta Stiktos Betta Stiktos is the rarest member of the Betta splendens complex, found only in the Mekong River basin in Cambodia around Stung Treng. The species was once thought to be extinct, but it has reappeared in the hobby through a handful of breeders. Stiktos look similar to smaragdina but have a smaller, stouter body, shorter fins, and a spotted dorsal fin. Their snake-scale pattern is less spread across the body and more prominent on the face. Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Compact and slender with a stout head. Smaller than most splendens-complex relatives, reaching about 1.1 inches (2.8 cm) standard length. Tail Shape: Exclusively roundtail, with short caudal and anal fins. Up to 9 rows of black transverse bars on the caudal fin and 5 rows of round black spots on the dorsal fin, which gives the species its "spotted" look. Scaling: Snake-scale pattern that is lighter on the body and much more prominent on the face and gill plates. Coloration: Brown to black body with iridescent green-blue in males, with possible red patches on the fins. Females are browner with faint green. Operculum: Black with a prominent, widespread snake-scale pattern of iridescent green-blue, strongest in males. Habitat (wild Betta stiktos): Native to the Mekong drainage in Stung Treng, Cambodia. Lives in swampy streams and ponds with dense vegetation and low oxygen, in still, slightly acidic water (pH 5 to 7.5) at 22 to 27°C. Uses its labyrinth organ to breathe air. Care Guide Temperament: Males are VERY aggressive and should be kept alone or with non-male betta tankmates. Multiple females can be kept together in a planted community tank if there is enough space. Max Size: 2 inches TDS: 60 to 200 (we keep them in soft, low-TDS water to match our wild bettas, but they tolerate a wider range) pH: 5.0 to 7.0 Temperature: 75 to 85°F (much better coloration above 78°F). Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets. Breeding: Bubblenester. The male builds a bubble nest under a floating leaf or plants and places the eggs in it after spawning. Remove the female once spawning is done. Eggs hatch in 24 to 48 hours, and the fry are free-swimming about 3 days later. Start them on infusoria, then move to baby brine shrimp. Keep the surface calm so the nest stays intact.
