IUCN Red List: N/A (this is a hybrid color form and does not occur in the wild) Origin: Captive bred Betta Imbellis "Copper" Betta Imbellis "Copper" is a hybrid color form of Betta imbellis, the crescent betta of the Betta splendens complex. Like all copper splendens types, it is genetically steel blue with a double copy of the metallic gene, which turns the wild green into a metallic copper. Imbellis are known for the red crescent at the edge of the tail and the red teardrop on the anal fin. Colors can shift with the fish's mood and the lighting. Genes for iridescence: blbl (steel blue) Genes for metallic: ++ Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Slender and elongated, with a slightly compressed body. Reaches about 2.3 inches (6 cm). Fins: Short, rounded caudal fin with a red crescent-shaped band along the edge. Red on the ventral and anal fins, including the red teardrop on the anal fin. Scaling: Metallic copper scales spread across a dark brown body. Coloration: Dark brown to black base with a metallic copper to gold shine and red fin accents. Males are the most intense. Females are duller and brownish. Operculum: Two metallic copper vertical bars on the gill covers in males. Females have faint markings. Habitat (wild Betta imbellis): Native to southern Thailand, Peninsular Malaysia and northern Sumatra, with introduced populations in Singapore. Lives in rice paddies, swamps, roadside ditches and ponds with dense vegetation and leaf litter, in warm, slightly acidic to neutral water (pH 4 to 7). Uses its labyrinth organ to breathe air. Care Guide Temperament: Males are VERY aggressive and should be kept alone or with non-male betta tankmates. Multiple females can be kept together in a planted community tank if there is enough space. Max Size: 2.3 inches TDS: 60 to 200 (we keep them in soft, low-TDS water to match our wild bettas, but they tolerate a wider range) pH: 4.0 to 7.0 Temperature: 75 to 85°F (much better coloration above 78°F). Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets. Breeding: Bubblenester. The male builds a bubble nest under a floating leaf or plants and places the eggs in it after spawning. Remove the female once spawning is done. Eggs hatch in 24 to 48 hours, and the fry are free-swimming about 3 days later. Start them on infusoria, then move to baby brine shrimp. Keep the surface calm so the nest stays intact.
