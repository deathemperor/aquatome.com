It lives close to Parosphromenus ornaticauda, sometimes in the same waters. It stands out from other licorice gouramis with its plain red-brown fins outlined in thin white-blue lines, and it is one of the easier species to keep and breed.Learn more from our friends at the Parosphromenus Project.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Small and slender. Fins: Males have plain red-brown unpaired fins with thin white-blue lines, and similar pelvic fins with whitish to blue filaments. Females have round tails. Coloration: Brown body. Females turn pale beige during courtship and are hard to tell from other round-tailed species. Habitat (wild Parosphromenus anjunganensis): Blackwater at the type locality measured pH 3.5 to 5.4, 22 µS/cm conductivity and 83°F. Care GuideTemperament: Peaceful and uncomplicated. Keep a pair in 5 gal, or a group of 3 to 6 in 10 gal with plenty of caves and leaf litter. They can share a tank with tiny, calm blackwater fish such as Boraras, or with small mouthbrooding bettas like albimarginata and channoides, but tankmates will eat any fry. Never mix different Parosphromenus species.Max Size: 1.4 inches (3.5 cm)TDS: 0 to 50pH: 3.5 to 5.5Temperature: 72 to 82°FDiet: Micropredator. Wild-caught fish usually take only live food. We currently feed live baby brine shrimp, daphnia and blackworms.Breeding: Breeds well under standard licorice gourami conditions. The male builds a modest foam nest, often in caves formed by old leaves, and courts head-down. Clutches are medium-sized, up to about 60 eggs.
