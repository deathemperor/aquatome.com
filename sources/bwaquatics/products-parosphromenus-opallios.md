"Sukamara". Known sites include the Sukamara area, the Jelai Bila basin, the Arut basin near Pangkalanbuun and the Benipah river. The name means "opal", for the shifting colors of males in breeding dress. It lives in some of the softest, most acidic water of any licorice gourami.Learn more from our friends at the Parosphromenus Project.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Small and slender; females often look slightly more compact. Fins: Males have whitish-turquoise edges on the unpaired fins and blue, spotted pelvic fins with light blue filaments. Females lack the bright fin edges and have a less pointed, often rounded dorsal fin. Coloration: Males in breeding dress turn very dark with red zones that vary by locality. Females are much paler. Habitat (wild Parosphromenus opallios): Heavily overgrown shallow margins of dark blackwater, measured at about 9 µS/cm with pH just under 4.0 and almost no hardness. Care GuideTemperament: Peaceful and shy. Keep a pair in 5 gal, or a group of 3 to 6 in 10 gal with plenty of caves and leaf litter. They can share a tank with tiny, calm blackwater fish such as Boraras. Never mix different Parosphromenus species, since they can hybridize.Max Size: 1.4 inches (3.5 cm)TDS: 0 to 50pH: 3.5 to 5.0Temperature: 72 to 82°FDiet: Micropredator. Wild-caught fish usually take only live food. We currently feed live baby brine shrimp, daphnia and blackworms.Breeding: Cave spawner with a head-down courtship. The male guards the eggs on the cave ceiling and tends the larvae until they swim free. Keep pH at 4.5 or lower with plenty of leaf litter, since the humic substances protect the eggs.
