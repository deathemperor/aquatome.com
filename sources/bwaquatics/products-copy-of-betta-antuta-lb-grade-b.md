"Antuta" LB, High QualityBetta sp. "Antuta" is an undescribed member of the unimaculata complex from Bulungan Regency in North Kalimantan, Indonesian Borneo. Unlike most bettas, it lives in clear, flowing highland streams rather than swamps. It is best known for the large, dragon-like iridescent scales on the head. This group comes from a locality designated LB and has a full blue mask and good body masking.High Quality fish are very good, with some flaws such as missing scales, thinner iridescence or less spread, but they still have good color throughout, including a full mask and body scaling.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Long and slender with a large, blunt head. Males grow larger and develop a broader head. Fins: Rounded caudal fin. Males develop longer fins as they mature. Scaling: Large, dragon-like iridescent scales on the head and gill plates. On the best males the iridescence also covers the body scales. Coloration: Brown to bronze body with blue-green iridescence that can look copper under some light. Females are browner, with an orange to brown glow on the belly. Habitat (wild Betta sp. Antuta): Clear, well-oxygenated highland streams in Bulungan. Care GuideTemperament: Aggressive. Best kept in a species-only tank, since they will eat any fish that fits in their mouth. Give a pair plenty of caves and cover to break line of sight. Recommended 1 pair per 10 gal, or 20 gal for breeding.Max Size: 4 to 5 inchesTDS: 60 to 100pH: 5.5 to 7.5Temperature: 72 to 82°F (they prefer cooler water, but warmer water brings out color and breeding behavior)Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets.Health: In our experience, fish in this complex are very prone to ich and velvet during the first few weeks after import, so watch new arrivals closely.Breeding: Paternal mouthbrooder. The female collects the eggs during spawning and passes them to the male, who holds them in his mouth for about 2 to 3 weeks. Keep the tank quiet and leave a holding male alone, since a disturbed male may swallow or spit out the brood. He releases fully formed fry that can eat baby brine shrimp right away.
