IUCN Red List: Least Concern Origin: Wild caught (Hau Giang province, Vietnam) Betta Imbellis "True Wild" Vietnam Betta Imbellis "True Wild" Vietnam are wild-caught Betta imbellis from Hau Giang province. Imbellis, the crescent betta, are known for the red trim on the caudal, anal and ventral fins and the two iridescent bars on the cheek plates. They are close relatives of Betta siamorientalis. These are not line-bred or deliberately hybridized like most farm imbellis, though wild populations may carry some splendens genes from fighter types released into the area in the past. Like all wild bettas, their natural color is metallic green with blue iridescence. Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Slender and elongated, with a slightly compressed body suited to moving through dense vegetation. Reaches about 2.4 inches (6 cm). Tail Shape: Short, rounded caudal fin with a red crescent-shaped band along the edge, which gives the species its common name. Fins are shorter and less ornate than in domestic splendens. Scaling: Metallic green scales with blue iridescence spread across a dark brown body. Coloration: Dark brown to black base with iridescent blue or green scales. Males show vivid red on the caudal, ventral and anal fins, strongest during courtship or display. Females are duller and brownish with muted red. Operculum: Two iridescent blue-green vertical bars on the gill covers in males, standing out against the dark body. Females have faint markings. Habitat (wild Betta imbellis): Found in slow-moving, shallow water across Southeast Asia, including southern Thailand, Peninsular Malaysia, northern Sumatra and southern Vietnam. Lives in rice paddies, swamps, roadside ditches and ponds with dense vegetation and leaf litter, in warm, slightly acidic to neutral water (pH 4 to 7). Uses its labyrinth organ to breathe air. Care Guide Temperament: Males are VERY aggressive and should be kept alone or with non-male betta tankmates. Multiple females can be kept together in a planted community tank if there is enough space. Max Size: 2.3 inches TDS: 60 to 200 (we keep them in soft, low-TDS water to match our wild bettas, but they tolerate a wider range) pH: 5.0 to 7.0 Temperature: 75 to 85°F (much better coloration above 78°F). Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets. Breeding: Bubblenester. The male builds a bubble nest under a floating leaf or plants and places the eggs in it after spawning. Remove the female once spawning is done. Eggs hatch in 24 to 48 hours, and the fry are free-swimming about 3 days later. Start them on infusoria, then move to baby brine shrimp. Keep the surface calm so the nest stays intact.
