It lives in extremely soft, acidic peat swamp forest in Kalimantan, Indonesia, where the water is dark with tannins, shallow, slow-moving and heavily shaded. Compared with the common Chocolate Gourami, it has sharper, more distinct crossbands and is often more sensitive. This is a species best suited for experienced blackwater aquarists.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Deep, compressed, leaf-shaped body with a pointed head. Fins: Rounded fins; males show a light edge on the anal and dorsal fins. Coloration: Rich chocolate-brown with distinct pale vertical crossbands, including one running from the dorsal fin down across the body. Habitat: Shaded peat swamp streams and pools. Care GuideTemperament: Peaceful and shy. Best in a species-only tank or with very small, peaceful blackwater species such as Boraras or tiny Parosphromenus. Avoid fast, competitive feeders and boisterous fish.Max Size: About 1.6 in standard length (about 2 in total length)Tank: Leaf litter, driftwood and botanicals, gentle flow, dense plants and dim light. Use a mature, stable blackwater tank.TDS: Under 80 to 100 ppmpH: 3.5 to 6.0Temperature: 77 to 84°F; consistency is criticalDiet: Small carnivore. Baby brine shrimp, daphnia, mosquito larvae and finely chopped bloodworms. Many wild fish refuse dry foods at first and need patient conditioning.Health: Very sensitive to swings in pH and hardness and does not tolerate hard or alkaline water long term. Stress, bright light or aggressive tankmates can cause quick decline. Acclimate slowly.Breeding: Mouthbrooder.
