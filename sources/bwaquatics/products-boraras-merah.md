It was first collected in the Jelai Bila basin near Sukamara, Central Kalimantan; "merah" is the local word for red. In the wild it lives in shallow, tannin-stained peat swamp streams thick with leaf litter and submerged roots, where the water is extremely soft and acidic. Males develop intense fiery red to orange color with darker body markings, especially in breeding condition; females are slightly larger and rounder with softer tones.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Tiny, slender rasbora. Fins: Red fins with dark markings in males. Coloration: Red to orange body with an oval dark blotch on the side (not a full stripe as in B. brigittae) and a dark spot at the tail base. Habitat: Shallow peat swamp streams with leaf litter and roots. Care GuideTemperament: Peaceful. Keep in groups of at least 8 to 10; groups of 15 or more show better color and schooling. Best with other tiny soft-water fish such as small Parosphromenus, Sundadanio or other Boraras.
