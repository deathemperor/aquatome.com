It is known only from a single blackwater swamp near Langgam, in the Sungai Kampar drainage about 40 km southeast of Pekanbaru, Riau, Sumatra. Horst Linke found it in 2008, and it was traded as "sp. Langgam" until it was named. The name means "crimson tail". It is told apart from the similar bintan and harveyi types by the bold black and red bands on its unpaired fins.Learn more from our friends at the Parosphromenus Project.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Elongated and slightly compressed. Fins: Diamond-shaped caudal fin with an almost triangular black patch. Turquoise pelvic fins with black filaments. Males have much more colorful and longer fins than females. Coloration: In males, the dorsal and anal fins run from a black base through a bright crimson band, a narrow iridescent blue band and a black band to a light blue edge. Aquarium-bred males show more red than wild fish. Similar Species: Parosphromenus tweediei is its closest relative but has a rounded tail. Habitat (wild Parosphromenus phoenicurus): Blackwater swamp measured at 80°F, pH 5.25 and only 7 µS/cm conductivity. Care GuideTemperament: Peaceful, but males can be territorial. Keep a group of 5 in 5 gal or larger, or a pair for breeding. They can share a tank with tiny, calm blackwater fish such as Boraras. Never mix different Parosphromenus species, since they can hybridize.Max Size: 1.1 inches (2.9 cm) for wild fish; aquarium-bred fish reach 1.6 inches (4 cm)TDS: 0 to 50pH: 4.0 to 6.0Temperature: 74 to 82°FDiet: Micropredator. They only eat live food, so this is an expert fish, not one for beginners. We currently feed live baby brine shrimp, daphnia and blackworms.Breeding: Cave spawner with a head-down courtship, like its relatives. Match the very low conductivity of its home water.
