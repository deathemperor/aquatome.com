The name means "abundant", for its high dorsal-fin ray count of 14 to 17. It is often confused with Betta burdigala, but uberis males show a green iridescent patch along the middle of the flank and usually have a slimmer body and shorter fins.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Slender and less robust than Betta burdigala. Fins: Long-based dorsal fin with 14 to 17 rays and a rounded caudal fin. Fins are shorter than those of Betta burdigala. Scaling: Small, even scales. On males, a group of scales in the middle of the flank is iridescent green, forming a distinct spot in many fish. Coloration: Deep red body. The green mid-lateral spot varies between individuals, and some wild fish have no spot at all. Habitat (wild Betta uberis): Peat swamp forest pools in Central Kalimantan with very soft, dark, acidic water. Care GuideTemperament: Moderately aggressive. Males can be territorial when setting up a breeding site, but because of their small size you can keep multiple pairs with enough space and cover. The male builds a small bubble nest at the surface, usually under a floating leaf, inside a floating cave or among plants, and places the eggs in it after spawning. Pairs can often stay together in a well-planted tank; remove the female if the male keeps chasing her. Eggs hatch in 24 to 48 hours, and the fry are free-swimming about 3 days later.
