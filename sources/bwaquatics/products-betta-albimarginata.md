IUCN Red List: Endangered (very high risk of extinction in the wild) Origin: Wild caught (Malinau, North Kalimantan) Betta Albimarginata "Malinau" Betta Albimarginata "Malinau" is a locality form of Betta albimarginata, the white-seam betta, a small mouthbrooding betta from northern Borneo. Displaying males are brick red, and every fin except the pectorals has a broad white edge with a broad black band inside it. The Malinau locality is known for especially clean, sharp contrast between the red, black and white. Males show their best color when ready to breed. It is a great first fish for anyone getting into mouthbrooding bettas. Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Small and elongated, with large eyes and a broad mouth. Reaches about 1.5 inches (3 to 4 cm) total length. Fins: Rounded caudal fin. Every fin except the pectorals is red with a broad black band and a broad white edge. On the tail, the black band runs across the full height of the fin, and the dorsal fin carries the same black and white band. Channoides have only a thin white edge on the dorsal and a tail band that stops short of the top. Coloration: Males are brick red. Females are mottled brown. Habitat (wild Betta albimarginata): Described from the Sebuku River basin in northern Borneo, now North Kalimantan, Indonesia. Lives in very shallow water (2 to 4 inches deep) along the edges of forest streams with moderate current, among plant roots and leaf litter. Care Guide Temperament: Somewhat territorial, but can be kept as pairs or small groups with enough space, visual barriers and hiding spots. We recommend one pair per 5 gallons. Avoid aggressive or fast-moving tankmates. Max Size: 1.5 inches TDS: 60 to 100 (can tolerate higher TDS, but does best in soft water) pH: 4.0 to 6.5 Temperature: 75 to 85°F. Cooler water promotes longevity, while warmer water brings out color and breeding behavior. Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets. Breeding: Paternal mouthbrooder. The male catches the eggs on his anal fin, the female picks them up and spits them to him, and he holds them in his mouth for about 10 to 21 days. Leave the holding male undisturbed, since a stressed male may swallow the brood. The fry are large enough to eat baby brine shrimp and microworms right away. Share Link View full details
