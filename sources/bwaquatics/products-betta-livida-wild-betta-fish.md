The name comes from the Latin lividus, "jealous", for its green eyes. Much of its range has been converted to oil palm and rubber, and Malaysian hobbyist groups have bred the species and released it back into the wild.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Small and slender. Fins: Rounded caudal fin. Males have longer dorsal fins than females. Scaling: Small, even scales. Scattered scales carry metallic yellow-green specks, and some fish have a small green lateral blotch that fades with age. Coloration: Dark red with metallic yellow-green specks, green reflective eyes and parallel vertical gold bars on the gill cover. Females are duller. The male builds a small bubble nest at the surface, usually under a floating leaf, inside a floating cave or among plants, and places the eggs in it after spawning. Pairs can often stay together in a well-planted tank; remove the female if the male keeps chasing her. Eggs hatch in 24 to 48 hours, and the fry are free-swimming about 3 days later.
