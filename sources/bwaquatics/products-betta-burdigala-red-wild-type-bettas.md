Its name comes from Burdigala, the Latin name for Bordeaux, for its deep wine-red color. Clearing of peat swamp for oil palm has left it Critically Endangered.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Rounded head and a thicker, more robust body than Betta uberis. Fins: Wide, broad unpaired fins, longer than those of Betta uberis. Scaling: Small, even scales. On males, blue and green iridescence is spread across many flank scales rather than concentrated in one spot. Coloration: Deep wine red with blue and green iridescence on the body and fins. No mid-lateral spot. Habitat (wild Betta burdigala): Flooded peat swamp forest and blackwater streams on Bangka Island. Care GuideTemperament: Moderately aggressive. The male builds a small bubble nest at the surface, usually under a floating leaf, inside a floating cave or among plants, and places the eggs in it after spawning. Pairs can often stay together in a well-planted tank; remove the female if the male keeps chasing her. Eggs hatch in 24 to 48 hours, and the fry are free-swimming about 3 days later.
