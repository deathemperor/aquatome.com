It shares some of its swamps with Parosphromenus anjunganensis, but the two use different niches. It is one of the two small, lean species in the genus along with Parosphromenus parvulus, and it is the only licorice gourami whose females take on the male's colors during courtship. Breeding it is considered the "high school" of the licorice gourami hobby.Learn more from our friends at the Parosphromenus Project.Note: photos are from a quick Google search until we get our own.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Small and lean. Fins: Dorsal and anal fins black with white spots and broad, bright white iridescent edges. The caudal fin has a red flame pattern. Coloration: Males in breeding dress have a very dark to black lower body and a reddish-brown upper half. Females are normally striped, but during courtship they switch to an almost identical male pattern, red tail flames included. Similar Species: Females in normal dress can be confused with Parosphromenus parvulus. Habitat (wild Parosphromenus ornaticauda): Typical black peat-water swamps, measured at pH 4.5 (as low as 4.1 in 2017), 39 µS/cm and 82°F. Care GuideTemperament: Peaceful but very secretive. Males can be territorial. Keep a group of 5 in 5 gal or larger, or a pair for breeding. Never mix different Parosphromenus species.Max Size: 1.4 inches (3.5 cm)TDS: 0 to 30 (conductivity at or below 40 µS/cm)pH: 3.5 to 5.5Temperature: 74 to 82°FDiet: Micropredator. They only eat live food at first and need to be trained to accept anything else; in our experience, adding dither fish helps them learn to take dry food. We currently feed live baby brine shrimp, daphnia and blackworms.Health: Imported fish are often weak and prone to bacterial infections and velvet, so acclimate slowly and watch new arrivals closely.Breeding: Cave spawner with a head-up courtship: the male zig-zags around the female, flashing his white fin edges. Offer caves with narrow entrances (about 0.6 inches), or the pair may spawn under leaves or floating rafts. Clutches are small, usually 10 to 20 eggs, and often get moved or disappear overnight.
