IUCN Red List: Endangered (very high risk of extinction in the wild) Origin: Wild caught (Samarinda, East Kalimantan) Betta Channoides "Samarinda" Betta Channoides "Samarinda" is a small mouthbrooding betta from the Mahakam River basin in East Kalimantan, Borneo. It is often called the "snakehead betta" because its large mouth and head resemble a Channa. Channoides are more robust and redder than albimarginata, and the easiest way to tell them apart is the dorsal fin: on channoides it is all red with no black. They are relatively peaceful for bettas, so they can be kept in pairs or small groups. Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Small but robust, with a large head and mouth. Reaches about 2 inches (5 cm). Fins: Rounded caudal fin. The dorsal fin is almost entirely red with a thin white edge. On the tail, the black band near the edge stops before it reaches the top of the fin, unlike albimarginata. Coloration: Males are vibrant red. Females are brownish yellow. Habitat (wild Betta channoides): Native to slow-moving, shallow streams and pools in the Mahakam River basin, East Kalimantan, with soft, acidic, tea-colored water from decaying leaves. Care Guide Temperament: Somewhat territorial, but males typically don't flare at or attack other fish on sight. Can be kept as pairs or small groups with enough space. We recommend one pair per 5 gallons. Max Size: 2 inches TDS: 60 to 100 (can tolerate higher TDS, but does best in soft water) pH: 4.0 to 6.5 Temperature: 75 to 85°F. Prefers cooler water, but warmer water brings out color and breeding behavior. Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets. Breeding: Paternal mouthbrooder. After spawning, the male carries the eggs in his mouth until the fry are free-swimming. Leave the holding male undisturbed, since a stressed male may swallow the brood.
