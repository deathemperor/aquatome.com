IUCN Red List: Data Deficient (not enough information at this time) Origin: Captive bred Betta Smaragdina "Wild Type" Betta Smaragdina "Wild Type" is the natural green form of Betta smaragdina, a hardy species in the Betta splendens complex from the Khorat Plateau of northeastern Thailand. Smaragdina are known for the snake-like scaling on the face and body, and this line shows the original metallic green with blue iridescence. They come from a harsher environment than most splendens and can handle lower temperatures, which makes them a great choice for beginners. Bred by a breeder in Vietnam. Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Slender and elongated, with a streamlined, slightly compressed body. One of the larger species in the splendens complex, reaching about 2.3 inches (6 cm). Fins: Usually spadetail, sometimes roundtail. The caudal fin has transverse bars and lacks the red crescent-shaped band seen in some other bettas. Fins are often longer than in other wild splendens types. Scaling: Each body scale is outlined, with a metallic green center, forming a snakeskin pattern from the gill plate to the tail base. The same snake-scale pattern continues onto the operculum. Coloration: Dark brown to black base with metallic green scales and blue iridescence. Males show red accents in the fins. Females are duller and brownish. Operculum: Snake-scale pattern of iridescent green or blue scales, with two parallel red or silvery vertical bars in males, brightest when flaring. Females have simpler, fainter markings. Habitat (wild Betta smaragdina): Native to northeastern Thailand, Laos and parts of Cambodia in the Mekong Basin. Lives in slow-moving, shallow water such as rice paddies, ponds, marshes and streams with dense vegetation. Prefers warm (24 to 30°C), slightly acidic to neutral (pH 5.5 to 7.5) water and uses its labyrinth organ to breathe air. Care Guide Temperament: Males are VERY aggressive and should be kept alone or with non-male betta tankmates. Multiple females can be kept together in a planted community tank if there is enough space. Max Size: 2.3 inches TDS: 60 to 200 (we keep them in soft, low-TDS water to match our wild bettas, but they tolerate a wider range) pH: 5.5 to 7.5 Temperature: 75 to 85°F (much better coloration above 78°F). Known to withstand temperatures as low as 60°F. Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets. Breeding: Bubblenester. The male builds a bubble nest under a floating leaf or plants and places the eggs in it after spawning. Remove the female once spawning is done. Eggs hatch in 24 to 48 hours, and the fry are free-swimming about 3 days later. Start them on infusoria, then move to baby brine shrimp. Keep the surface calm so the nest stays intact.
