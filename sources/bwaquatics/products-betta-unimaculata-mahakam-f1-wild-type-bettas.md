It is a mouthbrooder from eastern Borneo, and many East Kalimantan localities are sold as unimaculata or unimaculata types. These fish are first-generation tank raised, bred from wild-caught Mahakam parents.Physical Characteristics Body Shape: Long and slender with a large, blunt head. Adult males grow larger than females. Fins: Rounded caudal fin. Fully grown males have longer pelvic fins. Scaling: Adult males develop iridescent plating on the cheeks and gill plates, with scattered iridescent scales along the body. Coloration: Brown body with blue iridescence on the cheeks, body and fins. Females have less iridescence. Habitat (wild Betta unimaculata): Clear forest streams with flowing water; FishBase records pH 6.5 to 7.5 and 70 to 77°F. Care GuideTemperament: Aggressive. Best kept in a species-only tank, since they will eat any fish that fits in their mouth. Give a pair plenty of caves and cover to break line of sight. Recommended 1 pair per 10 gal, or 20 gal for breeding.Max Size: 5 inchesTDS: 60 to 100pH: 6.5 to 7.5Temperature: 72 to 82°F (they prefer cooler water, but warmer water brings out color and breeding behavior)Diet: Carnivorous. We currently feed live blackworms, frozen bloodworms and pellets.Health: In our experience, fish in this complex are very prone to ich and velvet during the first few weeks after import, so watch new arrivals closely.Breeding: Paternal mouthbrooder. The female collects the eggs during spawning and passes them to the male, who holds them in his mouth for about 2 to 3 weeks. Keep the tank quiet and leave a holding male alone, since a disturbed male may swallow or spit out the brood. He releases fully formed fry that can eat baby brine shrimp right away.
