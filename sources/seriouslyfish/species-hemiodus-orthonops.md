Hemiodus orthonops is a large, shoaling hemiodontid characin from Brazil, Paraguay and Argentina, where it inhabits floodplain rivers and lakes. Growing to around 250 mm SL and very active, it needs a tank of at least 120 cm with a well-fitting cover. Like its congeners it can be nervous when first introduced but usually settles over time, especially in a large group.

## Maintenance
Write a note
Edit
A biotope setup would consist, very simply, of a layer of a couple of inches of inert sand as substrate, with perhaps some driftwood branches and twisted roots scattered around. It can be a bit flighty, and some floating vegetation will help to make it less so. It is also equally at home in a fully planted setup. In either case, the water should be very well-oxygenated and make sure the tank has a well-fitting cover as the fish can leap surprising distances. It is sensitive to poor water conditions, so tank maintenance must be stringent.

## Diet
Write a note
Edit
Unfussy, and will accept most live, frozen and dried foods. Try to make sure it receives some greenstuffs in its diet, as it's reported to feed quite heavily on plant matter in nature.
## Behaviour and compatibility
Write a note
Edit
Very peaceful but it can intimidate much smaller or more quiet species with its activity and size. It makes an ideal shoaling species for the larger South American biotope setup, where it can be combined with similarly-sized characins, cichlids such as Geophagus, Satanoperca and Heros, catfish and Loricariids. It is a shoaling species by nature and should always be kept in a group.

## Notes
Edit
Hemiodus species can be very nervous when first introduced to the aquarium, but usually settle down over time, especially if a large number are being kept together. All of them look superficially similar, but can be distinguished by differences in patterning. None are particularly common in the hobby.
