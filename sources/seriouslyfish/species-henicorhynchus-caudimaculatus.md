Henicorhynchus caudimaculatus is a riverine cyprinid from the middle to lower Mekong basin and the Chao Phraya, in China, Laos, Thailand and Cambodia. It is most often collected in mid-to-large channels, although in the Mekong basin it also enters smaller tributaries and inundated fields. It has a pair of small maxillary barbels and 5-6 faint, thin stripes along each flank, and has also been placed in Cirrhinus, Bangana, Labeo and Gymnostomus in the last few decades.
## Etymology
Edit
caudimaculatus: from the Latin cauda, meaning 'tail', and maculatus, meaning 'spotted'.
## Distribution
Edit
Recorded from the middle to lower Mekong River basin in southern China (Yunnan province), Laos, Thailand, and Cambodia, plus the Chao Phraya watershed in central Thailand.
Type locality is 'Thailand: Lam Ton Lang, a tributary of Menam Sak [Mae Nam Pa Sak; Ban Lam Thong Lang, village northwest of Pakjong'.

## Notes
Edit
H. caudimaculatus can be distinguished from congeners by the following combination of characters: presence of a pair of small maxillary barbels, rostral barbels absent; 5-6 faint, thin, lateral stripes along each flank; body depth fits 3.5-3.8 times in SL; 34-35 lateral line scales.
Roberts (1997) placed Henicorhynchus species in Cirrhinus, and Kottelat (2001) treated Henicorhynchus as a valid genus again. Kottelat (2013) then made it a synonym of Gymnostomus and moved its species into that genus. Later authors have not followed him, and these species are placed in Henicorhynchus. Gymnostomus now holds only the Indian species G. ariza, G. fulungee and G. horai.
Henicorhynchus is a member of the tribe Labeonini within the putative cyprinid subfamily Cyprininae, or simply the subfamily Labeoninae (name varies with author). Yang et al. (2012) divide this grouping into four subtribes: Labeoina, Garraina, Osteochilina and Semilabeoina. Henicorhynchus is in the Osteochilina and the Indian species of Gymnostomus are in the Labeoina, so the two genera are not closely related.
Some Henicorhynchus species are common in mainstream rivers at certain times of year and extremely important in local fisheries during these periods.
