The Siamese mud carp, Henicorhynchus siamensis, is a cyprinid from the Mekong, Chao Phraya and Mae Klong basins in Laos, Thailand, Cambodia and Vietnam. Common in the Mekong main channel and larger tributaries, it moves into inundated zones to spawn in the wet season and is abundant in the massive seasonal migrations of cyprinids. It has a relatively deep, plain silver body and a large, broad head.
## Etymology
Edit
siamensis: 'from Siam' [Thailand].
## Distribution
Edit
Recorded from the middle to lower Mekong River basin in Laos, Thailand, Cambodia, and Vietnam, plus the Chao Phraya and Mae Klong watersheds in central and western Thailand.
Type locality is 'Bangkok, Thailand.'
## Habitat
Write a note
Edit
Common in the Mekong main channel and larger tributaries, but also recorded in smaller affluents and some impoundments and swamps, especially where aquatic vegetation grows thickly.
During the wet season it moves into inundated zones with standing water to spawn, and is abundant in the massive seasonal migrations of cyprinid fishes that occur in the mainstream Mekong river in December to February and May to July.

## Notes
Edit
H. siamensis can be distinguished from congeners by the following combination of characters: possession of 5½ lateral line scales; body relatively deep, depth fits 3.2-3.4 times in SL; head large and broad, width fits 5.5-6.7 times in SL; snout not or only weakly projecting; body plain silver in colour with no dark marking on caudal peduncle.
Roberts (1997) placed Henicorhynchus species in Cirrhinus, and Kottelat (2001) treated Henicorhynchus as a valid genus again. Kottelat (2013) then made it a synonym of Gymnostomus and moved its species into that genus. Later authors have not followed him, and these species are placed in Henicorhynchus. Gymnostomus now holds only the Indian species G. ariza, G. fulungee and G. horai.
Henicorhynchus is a member of the tribe Labeonini within the putative cyprinid subfamily Cyprininae, or simply the subfamily Labeoninae (name varies with author). Yang et al. (2012) divide this grouping into four subtribes: Labeoina, Garraina, Osteochilina and Semilabeoina. Henicorhynchus is in the Osteochilina and the Indian species of Gymnostomus are in the Labeoina, so the two genera are not closely related.
Some Henicorhynchus species are common in mainstream rivers at certain times of year and extremely important in local fisheries during these periods.
