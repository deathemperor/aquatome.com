Henicorhynchus entmema is a cyprinid from the middle to lower Mekong basin in Laos, Thailand and Cambodia, and the Chao Phraya and Mae Klong in Thailand. It is considered one of the most abundant species in the massive seasonal migrations of cyprinids in the mainstream Mekong, and enters inundated forests in the wet season. The body is relatively slender and plain silver, with no dark marking on the caudal peduncle, and the snout often projects strongly.
## Distribution
Edit
Recorded from the middle to lower Mekong River basin in Laos, Thailand, and Cambodia, plus the Chao Phraya and Mae Klong watersheds in central and western Thailand.
Type locality is 'Thailand: Chiang Rai Province: Mekok River near Chiang Rai'.
## Habitat
Write a note
Edit
This species is considered to be one of the most abundant species in the massive seasonal migrations of cyprinid fishes that occur in the mainstream Mekong river in December to February and May to July. Otherwise, it has been recorded in rivers and streams of all sizes down to around 2-3 m in width, with slow to rapid flow, and is known to enter inundated forests during the wet season.
## Notes
Edit
H. entmema can be distinguished from congeners by the following combination of characters: possession of 32-33 lateral line scales; 5½ scale rows between lateral line and base of dorsal-fin; body relatively slender, depth fits 3.7-4.0 times in SL; snout often projecting strongly; body plain silver in colour with no dark marking on caudal peduncle.
Roberts (1997) placed Henicorhynchus species in Cirrhinus, and Kottelat (2001) treated Henicorhynchus as a valid genus again. Kottelat (2013) then made it a synonym of Gymnostomus and moved its species into that genus. Later authors have not followed him, and these species are placed in Henicorhynchus. Gymnostomus now holds only the Indian species G. ariza, G. fulungee and G. horai.
Henicorhynchus is a member of the tribe Labeonini within the putative cyprinid subfamily Cyprininae, or simply the subfamily Labeoninae (name varies with author). Yang et al. (2012) divide this grouping into four subtribes: Labeoina, Garraina, Osteochilina and Semilabeoina. Henicorhynchus is in the Osteochilina and the Indian species of Gymnostomus are in the Labeoina, so the two genera are not closely related.
Some Henicorhynchus species are common in mainstream rivers at certain times of year and extremely important in local fisheries during these periods.
