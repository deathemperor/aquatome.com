Reganochromis calliurus is a cichlid endemic to Lake Tanganyika, where it usually swims at 60 metres depth or more over sand or mud. Its deep-water lifestyle makes it difficult to collect, and it is rarely seen in the hobby. A peaceful biparental mouthbrooder, it should be kept in pairs and not with boisterous species such as mbuna.

## Diet
Write a note
Edit
It will accept most foods offered but live and frozen varieties should form the bulk of the diet. Vegetable matter does not feature in the natural diet but any meaty fare offered should not be animal in origin.
## Behaviour and compatibility
Write a note
Edit
A peaceful fish that should not be kept with boisterous species such as mbuna, or other Tanganyikans with different dietary requirements. Good tankmates include Gnathochromis permaxillaris and Frontosa. It should be maintained in pairs and a large aquarium is required if more than one pair is to be kept as they become territorial once they have found a partner.

## Reproduction
Write a note
Edit
Possible but tricky. Bi-parental mouth-brooder. The breeding aquarium should be set up as suggested above. A pH of 8.0-9.0 and a temperature of 24 – 25.5 °C75 – 78 °F are ideal. We recommend the purchase of a group of at least 6-8 young fish and allowing things to develop naturally. You will know when a pair has formed as they will often be seen together in a cave and will become territorial towards conspecifics. Spawning occurs in the cave and both parents share brooding duties, often swapping the brood between themselves. They may also both hold fry at the same time if the brood is very large. They usually remain in the cave during this period. The free swimming fry are released after around 3 weeks and will accept brine shrimp nauplii or microworm from this point.
