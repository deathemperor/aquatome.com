Hemibarbus mylodon is a cyprinid endemic to the Korean peninsula, where it occurs throughout North Korea and South Korea. A predominantly riverine fish, it lives in clear, well-oxygenated running water over sand, gravel, rock or mud, and adults tend to form schools just above the substrate in faster-flowing sections.
## Etymology
Edit
Hemibarbus: from the Ancient Greek ἡμι- (hēmi-), meaning 'half', and the generic name Barbus, to which members appear similar.
mylodon: from the Ancient Greek, meaning 'grinder', and ὀδών (odṓn), meaning ‘toothed', in allusion to the large fourth pharyngeal tooth, which is molariform with a small, round excavation on the upper surface.
## Distribution
