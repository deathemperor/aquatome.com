Henicorhynchus ornatipinnis is a small cyprinid known only from the Mekong River system in Laos, Thailand and Cambodia. It appears to favour still or slow-moving, often temporarily-inundated, marginal habitats away from the major river channels. Barbels are completely absent, the caudal peduncle is very deep, and the pectoral, pelvic and anal fins are usually reddish-orange or rose-tinted.
## Etymology
Edit
ornatipinnis: from the Latin ornatus, meaning 'decorated, adorned', and pinna, meaning 'fin', in reference to the colourful fins in this species compared with other members of the genus.
## Distribution
Edit
Known only from the Mekong River system in Laos, Thailand, and Cambodia. It does not appear to have been recorded from Vietnam.
Type locality is 'Roadside ditch on highway 24 at kilometer 150 marker, 179 kilometers by road east of Nakorn Ratchasima, northeastern Thailand'.

## Notes
Edit
H. ornatipinnis can be distinguished from congeners by the following combination of characters: head small; snout relatively short and blunt; caudal peduncle very deep; pectoral, pelvic, and anal fins usually reddish-orange or rose-tinted; pelvic and anal fins often with white tips; barbels completely absent.
Roberts (1997) placed Henicorhynchus species in Cirrhinus, and Kottelat (2001) treated Henicorhynchus as a valid genus again. Kottelat (2013) then made it a synonym of Gymnostomus and moved its species into that genus. Later authors have not followed him, and these species are placed in Henicorhynchus. Gymnostomus now holds only the Indian species G. ariza, G. fulungee and G. horai.
Henicorhynchus is a member of the tribe Labeonini within the putative cyprinid subfamily Cyprininae, or simply the subfamily Labeoninae (name varies with author). Yang et al. (2012) divide this grouping into four subtribes: Labeoina, Garraina, Osteochilina and Semilabeoina. Henicorhynchus is in the Osteochilina and the Indian species of Gymnostomus are in the Labeoina, so the two genera are not closely related.
Some Henicorhynchus species are common in mainstream rivers at certain times of year and extremely important in local fisheries during these periods.
