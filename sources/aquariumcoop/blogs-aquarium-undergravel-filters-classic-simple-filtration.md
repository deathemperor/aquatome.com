Undergravel filters have been around for a very long time, and were commonplace in fish rooms and retail fish stores for many years. With recent technological advances in filtration, this simple, easy filtration method saw reduced popularity as aquascapers started using power filters like canisters, internal filters, and hang-on-backs. The newer filtration systems allowed for the use of delicate aquasoil and fine sand, however they added mechanical complexity and lots of equipment that needed to be hidden, as well as a high price tag. While undergravel filters must be used with gravel, and cannot be used with sand, soil, or other fine substrates, they are cost-effective, reliable, and low maintenance filters that can last the lifetime of your aquarium. How Do Undergravel Filters Work, and What are the Benefits? The way undergravel filters work is by using an uplift tube to create flow, much like a sponge filter does, attached to a grid of plates under a couple of inches of standard aquarium gravel. The water is drawn downwards through the gravel, which creates a surface area for beneficial bacteria that extends the full floor of the aquarium. Once the water is drawn through the gravel, it is siphoned upwards by the uplift tubes, and flows back out into the aquarium. Debris, detritus, and fish waste gets trapped in the gravel, leaving water crystal clear and free of toxic ammonia and nitrite. In addition to being incredibly effective, undergravel filters provide a gentle form of filtration that is one of the safest options available for long-finned bettas, tiny fry, and shrimp. Undergravel filters require very little in terms of maintenance, and can typically be maintained without removing any equipment from the aquarium. Cleaning this filter is as simple as a normal gravel vacuuming procedure, as you would when deep-cleaning any aquarium’s substrate. If you aren’t familiar with how to gravel vacuum an aquarium, you can find a detailed guide here. If your undergravel filter is being used with the air stones that came with it, you shouldn’t ever have to worry about cleaning the air stones, either, as the solid plastic construction resists mineral buildup and clogging. Their low maintenance requirements, customizability, and broad application potential make these undergravel filters an excellent choice for large fish rooms and heavily stocked tanks, in addition to being very easily hidden behind plants and hardscape. Just like sponge filters, this type of filtration runs on air, so you can use a battery backup air pump to have reliable filtration, even through a power outage of up to 40 hours. Can Undergravel Filters Be Used in Planted Tanks? It has long been rumored that undergravel filters are not very compatible with planted aquariums, however we have found that not to be the case. As long as the correct type of substrate is being used, plants can thrive in a tank with an undergravel filter, and this filtration method has the added benefit of constantly moving oxygenated, nutrient-rich water through plant roots. This can prevent root rot, increase nutrient accessibility, and create stronger root structures. There is a chance that your big Amazon sword, or dwarf aquarium lily, will root themselves firmly into the undergravel filter plates, so just keep that in mind when choosing placement for these plants. You may want to keep these deep-rooted plants in their original placements long-term, or placing them in spots with no undergravel plates, since they will be more difficult to remove and/or gravel vacuum around than plants with small root systems. Which Substrate Should I Choose for an Undergravel Filter? Choosing a type of gravel for use with an undergravel filter is wonderfully simple. Gravel that is at least 5mm on average will sit on top of the grates without falling through, making this the ideal size. Whether you choose a decorative, color-coordinated gravel from your local aquarium shop, or an inexpensive bulk bag of pea gravel from the hardware store, you have plenty of options that will look great, and function well with this type of filter. Keep in mind that the more textured the gravel is, the more surface area it will have for beneficial bacteria to grow. How Many Undergravel Filter Kits Will I Need? Each kit contains 20 Easy Undergravel Filter Plates. This means that one kit can work with 10 gallon tanks, 20 gallon, 29 gallon, and more. 9 plates can cover the bottom of a 10 gallon aquarium, which means that one kit can be utilized for two 10 gallon aquariums with one uplift tube for each. For larger tanks where you are wanting full coverage, we recommend two kits. Each plate measures 5.5” x 2.75”. You can use the following formula to find out how many plates you need to cover the entire bottom of a larger aquarium: Length of tank in inches, divided by 5.5 (round the result down to the nearest whole number) = A Width of tank in inches, divided by 2.75 (round the result down to the nearest whole number) = B Multiply A x B and that will give you the number of plates needed to cover the entire bottom of the aquarium. If you only plan to cover a portion of the bottom of the aquarium, only measure the area you want undergravel plates to cover.
