Most people starting off in the aquarium hobby often experience difficulty in choosing the best aquatic plants for their aquariums. While others opt for artificial plants because of the common misconception that floating plants are hard to look after. However, this is not true; floating plants are great for aquascaping and they are very easy to maintain in the tank.
Floating plants are more lively, they have roots that hang onto the water surface for growth and absorption of nutrients, and they are known to provide a lot of benefits to tank inhabitants.
## Benefits of Floating Plants
- Cover: Floating plants provide cover/shade for fish and shrimps. These plants act as shelter and hiding place for tank inhabitants.
- Oxygenation: One of the main functions of floating plants, when introduced into the tank, is oxygen generation. They help to provide extra oxygen to the tank water which is beneficial to the inhabitants. Also, a high level of oxygen in the tank helps to reduce the presence of harmful toxins.
- Aesthetic value: Most floating plants are attractive and can add great aesthetic value to your aquarium. These plants will not only serve as a decorative means but will create a natural-like look to your tank and you will definitely love it.
- Filtration: Floating plants are great filters. The plants use their well-adapted roots in providing biological and chemical filtration. They absorb carbon dioxide created by tank inhabitants and other detritus from the water thus keeping the water clean and free from bacteria.
In addition, they also help in reducing excessive nitrate levels in the water and out-competing excessive algae formation. You can read more about it in my article “Aquarium Floating Plants. Pros and Cons”.
Here, we will talk about the top 7 floating plants for beginners. This guide will help you choose the best floating plants to introduce into your tank and how to care for them.

## 1. Frogbit:
Its scientific name is Limnobium laevigatum (Amazon Frogbit). It is native to South and Central America. This plant inhabits in slow-moving freshwater bodies like ponds, marshes, ditches, and swamps. Frogbit is a popular aquatic plant in the aquarium hobby, commonly used for ornamental purposes. They are fast growers and can cover a large space in the aquarium within a short time.
Frogbit Characteristics:
- Frogbit has a round leaf structure and a vibrant green color
- Large rosettes
- This floating plant grows leaves that lie flat in the water surface. The leaves are smooth and unmarked, adapted for buoyancy due to its bloated underside
- They are very attractive and can grow up to 20 inches in height, 0.5 to 3 inches in width
- Flowers are small, white, and unisexual
- Possession of long branching roots
Frogbit Optimal Requirements:
- Water pH: 6.0 – 7.5.
- Hardness: Soft to moderately hard water.
- Temperature: 64-80 degrees Fahrenheit (18 – 26 C).
- Lighting: Moderate to high lighting.
Frogbit Propagation:
The reproduction of this plant species is through flower pollination and sexual production.

## 2. Dwarf Water Lettuce:
Dwarf water lettuce is an amazing floating plant that is a great addition to any aquarium or tank. This plant grows averagely fast and does well with little or no fertilization at all. They are native to Africa and are quite popular in the aquarium hobby because of their versatility & resilience.
Dwarf water lettuce Characteristics:
- Large, spongy, thick broad leaves and rounded structure.
- Leaves possess parallel veins and are covered in short hairs which form basket-like structures that trap air bubbles for buoyancy
- Overall pale green coloration
- 10 inches or 25 inches in diameter
- Long stringy root structure
- Flowers (dioecious), hidden in the center of the plant
Dwarf water lettuce Optimal Requirements:
- Water pH: 6.0-7.5
- Hardness: Soft to moderately hard water.
- Temperature: 71-82 degrees Fahrenheit (22 – 28C).
- Lighting: Moderate lighting.
Dwarf water lettuce Propagation:
Propagation in dwarf water lettuce is through sexual and asexual reproduction.
Care level: Moderate

## 3. Red Root Floater:
As the name suggests, the Red root floater (Phyllanthus fluitans) is a floating plant with distinct red roots commonly used as an aquarium plant. It is attractive and the red color is more pronounced under high lighting conditions.
Red root floater (Phyllantus fluitans) originates from South America, the Amazon River Basin, and extends into Central America. This fast-grower has adapted to become a floating plant in a wide range of living environments.
It desires rich nutrient content in the water, especially iron for optimum growth and development of healthy red roots and colorful reddish hue leaves.
Red root floater Characteristics:
- Bright red roots and underside
- Long trailing root system
- Green leaves that develop a reddish hue tinge in intense lighting conditions
- Brittle creeping stems
- They possess tiny unisexual flowers
Red root floater Optimal Requirements:
- Water pH: 6.5-7.5
- Hardness: Soft to moderately hard water.
- Temperature: 72-80 degrees Fahrenheit (22-26C).
- Lighting: Moderate to high lighting.
Red root floater Propagation:
Red root floater propagates from the stalks, the plants are known to branch out easily and divide into multiple parts that grow at a fast rate. They propagate very easily with little or no assistance, however, they will need iron supplementation and intense light to grow better.

## 4. Salvinia Auriculata:
Salvinia auriculata is a free-floating fern that grows rapidly on the water surface. This plant is indigenous to South and Central America and is commonly used in the aquarium hobby. It is capable of extremely fast growth, its high leaf and branch densities allow it to form continuous dense mats on the water surface.
Salvinia auriculata is non-rooted macrophyte that prefers slow-moving water bodies. It can be commonly found in a wide variety of aquatic habitats including lakes, ponds, streams, aquariums, marshes, and rivers.
Salvinia auriculata Characteristics:
- Diverse growth forms, from small flat leaves to mat forms with large, crowded leaves (Depending on environmental conditions)
- Flat floating leaves
- Size: small-sized, 1-3cm
- Submerged leaves are root-like, whitish, and have hair-like projections
- Creeping stems
- Possession of sporocarps (responsible for the production of spores)
Salvinia auriculata Optimal Requirements:
- Water pH: 6.0-8.0
- Hardness: Soft to moderately hard water required.
- Temperature: 65 – 80 degrees Fahrenheit (18 – 26C).
- Lighting: Moderate to high lighting.
Propagation
Salvinia auriculata reproduces asexually through the fragmentation process. Any part of a rhizome that buds and detaches can form a newly independent plant.

## 5. Duckweed:
This is a tiny floating plant, it is regarded as one of the smallest flowering plants in existence. Duckweed (Lemnaceae) can easily be found living within a wide variety of aquatic environments but slow-moving water favors it more. It requires little or no care and will thrive in relatively every kind of tank set-up. Duckweed is an excellent floating plant that can act as a cover for fish in the aquarium and shelter for newborn fry.
This plant is a great option for those new to the aquarium hobby, it grows quickly and is compatible with diverse tank inhabitants.
Duckweed Characteristics:
- Has one to three flat leaves/fronds
- Air pockets that allow it to float or remain just below the water’s surface
- Leaf size less than an inch
- Single root protruding from each frond (about 1 – 5.55 inches)
- Overall bright light green coloration
- Fast growers
Duckweed Optimal Requirements:
- Water pH: 6.5 – 7.5
- Hardness: Soft to moderately hard water.
- Temperature: 60-85 degrees Fahrenheit (15 – 30C).
- Lighting: Moderate lighting.
Duckweed Propagation:
Duckweeds propagate sexually by forming chains of new stems from vegetative buds.

## 6. Water Sprite:
Scientific name – Ceratopteris thalictroides. Water sprite is a fast-growing beginner plant that is well suited for a wide range of aquarium and water types. Water sprite grows in swamps, ponds, marshes, and other slow-moving water bodies. For the aquarium hobby, Water sprite is a great choice and an undemanding plant that thrives successfully in a well-planted tank.
Water sprite Characteristics:
- Lace-like leaf structure
- Narrow green leaves
- Pale or slightly bright green coloration
- Short and fleshy rhizomes
- Has proliferous or dormant buds with large scales
- Long floating roots for nutrient absorption
Water sprite Optimal Requirements:
- Water pH: 6.5-8.0
- Hardness: Soft to moderately hard water required.
- Temperature: 60-80 degrees Fahrenheit (15 – 26C).
- Lighting: Low to moderate lighting.
Water sprite Propagation:
Just like other ferns, Water sprite reproduces when baby plantlets form on the parent plant, which is then released into the water column. You will notice plenty floating plantlets in the aquarium which will grow and develop into full plants within a short time.
One can also propagate Water sprite by replanting stem trimmings in the substrate or leave them floating in the water. They will develop roots and the growth cycle continues.

## 7. Water Wisteria:
Water wisteria is a floating plant adored by beginners because of its fast-growing ability and undemanding nature. This plant is native to countries in Southern Asia – India, Bangladesh, Bhutan, and Nepal where it grows and reproduces on marshy habitats and shallow water bodies.
This plant can be grown submerged or floating above the water surface. Water wisteria is easy to introduce into the aquarium, it is a versatile plant and thrives in a wide variety of tank conditions.
Water wisteria Characteristics:
- Bright green leaves with serrated margins, oval and lace-like
- Has grandular hairs on leaves
- Thin root system for attachment to substrate and absorption of nutrients
- Height (20-50cm)
- Ability to effect change in the structure of the leaves
- Blue–violet flowers
Water wisteria Optimal Requirements:
- Water pH: 6.0-8.0
- Hardness: Soft to moderately hard water.
- Temperature: 20 – 27 C (68 – 82 F).
- Lighting: Moderate lighting condition.
Water wisteria Propagation:
Propagation is through stem cuttings. As a beginner, all you have to do is to cut parts of the stem and grow them in a nutrient-rich substrate. These cuttings will develop their own roots and grow into new plants after a while.

## Quarantine Any New Plant
Do not forget to quarantine any new plants before putting them into your tank!
- They can have parasites, pests like snails, or even predators (dragonfly, damsefly nymphs, etc.).
- They can be treated with chemicals (pesticide) to remove parasites, snails, etc. However, these chemicals are extremely poisonous to shrimp and invertebrates.
