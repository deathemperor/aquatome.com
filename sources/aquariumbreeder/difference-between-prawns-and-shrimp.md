Quick Answer: Shrimp have curved bodies, plate-like gills, and claws on their first two pairs of legs. Prawns have straighter bodies, branching gills, and claws on three pairs of legs. In aquariums, we generally keep shrimp (Neocaridina/Caridina). Prawns (Macrobrachium) are too large and aggressive for most tanks.

Forget cooking shows. In the aquarium hobby, “prawn” usually means large Macrobrachium species — not the food you bread and fry. These are semi-aggressive, need brackish water for breeding, and will eat your cherry shrimp. If you want peaceful tank cleaners, stick with Neocaridina or Caridina.

## Key Differences Between Prawns and Shrimp
Important: Before I start I need to clarify that names like shrimp and prawn are not scientific terms. We use them as common names and terms of convenience. Therefore, when we are discussing this question we are actually talking about their actual taxa even though we use scientifically inappropriate terminology.Also, these terms can be misleading. For example, Spot prawns (Pandalus platyceros, also called California spot prawn) are shrimp. At the same time, Ridgeback shrimp (Sicyonia ingentis) are actually prawns.
## 1. Scientific Suborder
Both prawns and shrimp fall under the term “decapod”, which means that they have 10 legs. However, the two species fall under different subcategories or suborders.
Shrimp belong to the Pleocyemata suborder — same as crayfish, lobsters, and crabs. Prawns belong to Dendrobranchiata.

## 2. Anatomy
Despite looking quite similar, prawns and shrimp have very different anatomy, which further argues the point that they are not the same.
Body shape. The thorax of shrimp overlaps their head and abdomen, while that of prawns overlaps only on the bottom portion of each body segment. Since prawns overlap in fewer places, their bodies are more straight and, to the naked eye, appear to have fewer segments – overlapping or otherwise.
Because of the way the segments overlap, shrimp have a much more versatile range of motion and are much more flexible than prawns.
For example, shrimp can bend into sharp, severe angles and difficult positions, much like a human dancer can. Prawns can only bend in certain ways and cannot bend to the same extent that shrimp can.
Legs. In addition, the two subspecies have legs that are slightly different. Prawns have three pairs of claw-like legs. Shrimp have claws on two pairs — the first two legs. Grab a dwarf shrimp with tweezers and you’ll see both front claws grab back. Prawns have longer legs, as well.
Gill structure. In shrimp, the gills have a thin-plated structure, basically, they consist of flat, layered arrangements. Whereas in prawns the gill structure is branching.

## 3. Fertilization and Reproduction
In shrimp, males leave sperm at the genital opening of female shrimp using their appendix masculine. Therefore, eggs are fertilized as they pass from the ovaries to the outside of the body (under her tail).
Prawns have internal fertilization of eggs.
Shrimp carry their fertilized eggs externally – more specifically, attached to the swimmerets on the underside or “belly” portion of their body.
Prawns often do not carry their eggs as shrimp do; instead, they release them into the water and leave them to grow unattended.

## 4. Size
When it comes to size, prawns tend to be larger than shrimp. Of course, depending on the species, this idea might not be accurate.
Interesting fact: Currently, there are more than 3000 Caridean species (True shrimp). One of the small ones are Opae Ula Shrimp (Halocaridina rubra). These small shrimp are rarely longer than 0.6 inches (~ 1.5 cm) in length, whereas Macrobrachium rosenbergii can grow up to 12 inches (over 30 cm).
Nonetheless, in most cases, though, it’s a very valid differentiating point between the two crustaceans.
## 5. Habitat
There is an opinion that habitat (water preference) is another criteria that can be used to differentiate prawns and shrimp.
Personally, I do not think that this criterion has a really strong point.
Shrimp and prawns can both be found in fresh or saltwater all over the world. That is why the difference between them is a little bit slim.
It is said that most species of shrimp are saltwater, with only around 20% of all known species residing in freshwater.
Prawns, unlike shrimp, are found mostly in freshwater although, as we said, they can be found in both types of water.
I have done my best to find any additional data on this matter. Unfortunately, nobody really knows how many shrimp and prawn species live in fresh or saltwater.
## FAQ
Can I keep prawns with dwarf shrimp?
No. Macrobrachium will eat Neocaridina. Period.
Do prawns need brackish water?
Most species need brackish water for breeding. Some can live in freshwater as adults, but won’t reproduce.
Which is better for beginners: shrimp or prawns?
Dwarf shrimp (Neocaridina/Caridina). They’re peaceful, small, and breed easily in freshwater. Prawns need large tanks and experience.
## Bottom Line
Prawns and shrimp aren’t interchangeable. Different gills, different claws, different care requirements.
For 99% of home aquariums: stick with dwarf shrimp. They’re easier, safer, and actually fit in nano tanks.
If you insist on prawns — research Macrobrachium species first. You’ll need 55+ gallons and experience dealing with semi-aggressive inverts.
