You read what a fish's sources say about where it lives in the wild, and list the countries and places they name, each with the sentence that says so. A program checks every entry against the text and drops any it can't find there.

Use only the text below. Never add a country or a place from your own knowledge, even one you are sure of: a source that says nothing about where the species lives gives no entries.

countries: one entry per country each source names (two sources naming one country are two entries).
- code: the country's ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code.
- status:
  - native: the source says the species occurs there naturally: its natural range or distribution, "native to", "endemic to", "occurs in", "found in", "known from", its type locality.
  - introduced: the source says it was introduced, released or escaped there, or lives there as a feral or established population.
  - doubtful: the source says records from there are misidentifications, refer to another species, are unconfirmed or doubtful, or that it does not occur there.
  - A statement about part of a country doesn't make the whole country introduced or doubtful: "records from eastern Thailand refer to other species" beside "occurs throughout central Thailand" is Thailand native, once.
  - Where the fish is bred, farmed, collected for export, sold or kept is not where it lives: no entry. Nor is a country named only in a common name ("the 'Pakistani' loach").
- source: the source's name as its heading gives it ("seriouslyfish"), without the section.
- quote: the one sentence (or the one quick-facts line) of that source's text that says it, copied exactly, character for character.
- words: the country's name exactly as the quote writes it: its name ("Thailand"), a former name ("Siam", "Burma", "Zaire") or its adjective ("Brazilian" in "the Brazilian state of Amapá").
- Only a country the quote names. A place that isn't a country (Borneo, Sumatra, New Guinea, Lake Malawi, the Amazon, the Mekong, a province, a city) gives no country entry, even when you know which country it is in: it goes in regions.

regions: the river systems, lakes and islands each source names as where the species lives naturally, and wider natural regions such as a peninsula or a mountain range.
- name: a short proper name as the quote writes it ("Rio Negro", "Mekong", "Lake Malawi", "Borneo", "Kalimantan", "Chao Phraya", "Orinoco", "Malay Peninsula"). Not a country or a continent; not a province, state, district, town or single collecting site; not a description ("blackwater streams", "rice paddies", "the north of South America"); not a place it was introduced to.
- quote: the one sentence of that source's text that names it, copied exactly.
- country: the code of the country the quote puts it in, when the quote names that country ("Koshi" in "Nepal (Koshi, Narayani and Karnali rivers)" is NP); "" when it doesn't.
- At most 6 in all: the main basins and rivers first, a tributary only where the source names no wider one.
