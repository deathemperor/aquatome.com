Translate these YouTube video titles into {{LANG}}, for an aquarium handbook read in {{COUNTRY}}. Each line gives the fish, plant or product the page is about, in brackets, then the video's title.

{{STYLE}}

- Say only what the title says. Add no claim, number, promise or detail it doesn't make.
- Short and natural, as {{LANG}}-speaking hobbyists would write a title: at most about 80 characters, not counting the brackets this asks for, and not word for word.
- A title in a language other than English is read in that language's hobby sense, not through English (Italian "allevamento" is keeping, "riproduzione" breeding).
- Leave out who made or sells the video: a channel's or a seller's name, a show whose name says nothing about the fish ("FishFacts part 351") and any stock or listing code. The page credits the video, and pages never name a channel. Never translate such a name. A brand stays only as the maker of a product the video is about. A channel's name that sounds like praise ("the king of DIY") is still a name: leave it out. Such a label is not a new subject, a new tank or a style: "Mein Aquarium 14" is "my aquarium, part 14", with "my" as {{LANG}} keepers write it.
- The fish: first see whether the title names this exact fish or plant, or only its group. A scientific name in the title names this exact one: write the page's name as given in brackets, then the scientific name in brackets. Where the title names only a group ("Pleco", "Betta", "Neocaridina shrimp", "Rummynose" for any of them), write the group's word without whatever in the page's name makes it this one kind (a person's name, a colour, a place), wherever it sits, and in lower case where {{LANG}} writes a common noun so. One page calls one fish by one word. Never coin a common name, and never keep an English one the page doesn't use: write the page's name, the group's word or the scientific name. A strain or colour form the title names ("red tuxedo", "blue tiger") is kept as the trade writes it, right after the fish's name and before its bracket. A half that only renames the fish with a name {{LANG}} keepers don't use is left out, and a place in it is named as {{LANG}} readers know it; where a half names what the video is about ("a Blue Neon Rasbora care guide"), write the page's name in its place and keep it. Keep a brand, a product's model or a scientific name as it is.
- One title in one piece. Where the title has two halves (split by "|", " - ", ":" or a full stop), say both as one:
  - a half that praises or renames the fish or the product becomes describing words beside its name, or, where {{LANG}} can't, its apposition after one comma;
  - a half that tells what happened becomes a sentence with the fish as its subject, and every action the title gives the fish stays ("dance", "hunting");
  - halves that promise the same thing ("everything you need to know", "all about", "complete guide", "care guide" and "how to keep") keep one, and so do two names for one place ("in nature" and "its habitat");
  - "a beginner's guide" becomes the title's reader ("what a beginner should know"), never a second half.
  No dash, colon or bar between them. Thai joins its halves with a space, never a comma, and with "เป็น" only where it reads as a statement. A plain "X is Y" is fine; never "which is", and never "is" opening a list.
- A describing word stays joined to the noun it describes, where {{LANG}} puts it: before a bracket, or after it where {{LANG}} puts praise last. A size follows the tank word directly, before the fish ("akuarium 90 liter", "aquascape 55 galon … untuk ikan pedang"), never after the fish's name, and the fish is linked to its tank by the word {{LANG}} uses (Thai "เลี้ยง", Malay "untuk").
- Write each sentence so it can be read one way only: who does what stays clear ("shreds herring with its tank mates" is "with its tank mates, shreds herring" where the first could mean it shreds them). A word the hobby keeps in English ("timelapse") stays English.
- Keep each word's strength, in the words a keeper would type in their own video title, not a dictionary's or a news headline's: a wow stays a wow, with no "very" added, and each word keeps its sense ("biggest" is size, not greatness).
- Capitals only on the first word, a name and a brand, never on ordinary words mid-title ("part", "my aquarium") as an English title has them.
- Keep sizes as the title gives them; where it already gives litres, keep its figure and add nothing. After gallons given alone, add litres in brackets ("40 gallon (about 150 litres)"): {{TANKS_EN}}. After inches, add centimetres in brackets, in whole centimetres. Numbers as {{LANG}} writes them, a space before a unit, the unit written out ("liter", never "L"), and feet only as centimetres; gallon as {{LANG}}'s dictionary spells it (Indonesian "galon", Malay "gelen").
- A part or episode number takes {{LANG}}'s word for it (Vietnamese "phần", Indonesian "bagian", Malay "bahagian", Thai "ตอนที่"), and goes last: a title never ends on a number by itself.
- Keep "planted" as the hobby's {{LANG}} word for a tank with live plants (Vietnamese "bể thủy sinh", Thai "ตู้ไม้น้ำ", Indonesian and Malay "aquascape"), never the English word "planted". That word stands for the whole tank, so no other word for tank beside it.
- Leave out hashtags, emoji, shouting capitals, exclamation marks, and words asking the viewer to watch or subscribe. No "your" ("for your tank" is "for the tank"), and name the tank once: where the title already names a tank or aquarium, leave "in your tank" out. No quotes, and no full stop at the end.
- If the title says almost nothing, only a code or a channel's name, write only the name in brackets. Never invent what the video shows.
- If what you'd write is only a scientific name, begin it with the {{LANG}} word for fish or plant, so the title is never left with no {{LANG}} word in it.
- One item per title, its n the title's number.
