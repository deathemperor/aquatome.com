Translate these YouTube video titles into {{LANG}}, for an aquarium handbook read in {{COUNTRY}}. Each line gives the fish, plant or product the page is about, in brackets, then the video's title.

{{STYLE}}

- Say only what the title says. Add no claim, number, promise or detail it doesn't make.
- Short and natural, as {{LANG}}-speaking hobbyists would write a title: at most about 80 characters, not word for word.
- Leave out who made or sells the video: a channel's or a seller's name, a show whose name says nothing about the fish ("FishFacts part 351") and any stock or listing code. The page credits the video, and pages never name a channel. Never translate such a name. A brand stays only as the maker of a product the video is about.
- The fish: first see whether the title names this exact fish or plant, or only its group. A scientific name in the title names this exact one: write the page's name as given in brackets, then the scientific name in brackets. Where the title names only a group ("Pleco", "Betta", "Neocaridina shrimp", "Rummynose" for any of them), write the group's word without whatever in the page's name makes it this one kind (a person's name, a colour, a place), wherever it sits, and in lower case where {{LANG}} writes a common noun so. One page calls one fish by one word. Keep a brand, a product's model or a scientific name as it is.
- One title in one piece. Where the title has two halves (split by "|", " - ", ":" or a full stop), say both as one:
  - a half that praises or renames the fish becomes describing words beside its name, or, where {{LANG}} can't, its apposition after one comma;
  - a half that tells what happened becomes a sentence with the fish as its subject;
  - halves that promise the same thing ("everything you need to know", "all about", "complete guide") keep one.
  No dash, colon or bar between them, and in Thai no space between halves. A plain "X is Y" is fine; never "which is", and never "is" opening a list.
- A describing word goes right beside the noun it describes, before any bracket, or opens the title ("the beauty of…", "care, biology and feeding of…"). After a bracket, only what the fish does, or where, may follow.
- Keep each word's strength, in the words a keeper would type in their own video title, not a dictionary's or a news headline's: a wow stays a wow, with no "very" added.
- Capitals only on the first word, a name and a brand, never on ordinary words mid-title ("part", "my aquarium") as an English title has them.
- Keep sizes as the title gives them. After gallons, add litres in brackets ("40 gallon (about 150 litres)"): {{TANKS_EN}}. After inches, add centimetres in brackets, in whole centimetres. Numbers as {{LANG}} writes them; gallon as {{LANG}}'s dictionary spells it (Indonesian "galon", Malay "gelen").
- A part or episode number takes {{LANG}}'s word for it (Vietnamese "phần", Indonesian "bagian", Malay "bahagian", Thai "ตอนที่"), and goes last: a title never ends on a number by itself.
- Keep "planted" as the hobby's {{LANG}} word for a tank with live plants (Vietnamese "bể thủy sinh", Thai "ตู้ไม้น้ำ", Indonesian and Malay "aquascape"), never the English word "planted". That word stands for the whole tank, so no other word for tank beside it.
- Leave out hashtags, emoji, shouting capitals, exclamation marks, and words asking the viewer to watch or subscribe. No "your" ("for your tank" is "for the tank"), and name the tank once: where the title already names a tank or aquarium, leave "in your tank" out. No quotes, and no full stop at the end.
- If the title says almost nothing, only a code or a channel's name, write only the name in brackets. Never invent what the video shows.
- If what you'd write is only a scientific name, begin it with the {{LANG}} word for fish or plant, so the title is never left with no {{LANG}} word in it.
- One item per title, its n the title's number.
